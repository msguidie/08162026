==============================================================================================================

### lens
Maximize P(a working, strong-enough agent from ONE autonomous implementation pass), minimizing moving parts — but I read "minimize moving parts" as minimizing *hand-tuned, mode-specific, unverifiable* surface area, not minimizing lines of code. The single largest source of one-shot failure in this project is not algorithmic sophistication; it is (a) a rules mirror that silently diverges from server/gameLogic.js, and (b) a hand-designed multi-mode reward function that nobody can validate. A design that needs zero reward shaping and whose search can be validated before any learning exists is *lower* risk than a "simple" PPO pipeline that requires a 400-line potential function retuned for five win conditions. Compute is not the binding constraint here: on 4xA100 + 128 cores we can generate more self-play games in one day than every profiled Splendor project generated in total. So I optimize for strength ceiling and verifiability, not FLOP efficiency.

### recommended_algorithm
AlphaZero-style policy + PER-SEAT VALUE VECTOR network, trained by determinized-PUCT MCTS self-play, with one mode-conditioned network covering 2p/3p/4p individual, 1v2 and 2v2, and MCTS also at deployment.

Concretely:
- Search: PUCT MCTS, tree keyed by ACTION PATH (open-loop), with a fresh determinization sampled per simulation from a small fixed universe set (K=4-8 in training, K=16-32 at inference). NO explicit chance nodes. NO full ISMCTS.
- Targets: policy = root visit distribution (with KataGo forced playouts + policy-target pruning); value = 0.5*(root Q of most-visited child) + 0.5*(terminal outcome vector). Zero reward shaping.
- Value representation: a length-4 vector (seat-relative, slot 0 = acting player, padded + masked for 2p/3p), backed up by rotation. This is the one decision that makes all five modes work with one net.
- Playout Cap Randomization: 25% of moves get a 600-sim "full" search with Dirichlet noise + forced playouts and ARE recorded; 75% get a 120-sim search with no noise and are NOT recorded.
- Self-play: 90% pure self-play (all seats = current weights, continuously refreshed), 10% mixed games with 1-2 seats drawn from an anchor-pinned historical pool (only current-net seats are recorded). Champion gating is used for *deployment selection and Elo logging only*, never to stall generation.

Rejected as primary: plain MaskablePPO self-play + league (option 1); pure hybrid PPO-then-search (option 3); MuZero (we have a perfect simulator — learned dynamics is pure added risk); DQN/Rainbow variants (BreckEmert's DDQN champion was beaten 29-1 by 150-sim search on its own weights).

Retained as an explicit, cheap fallback: if Phase 2 shows MCTS throughput is unaffordable, swap ONLY the training loop to MaskablePPO on the identical env/obs/action/mask/eval code, and keep MCTS at inference. That is a small diff because ~80% of the work is shared infrastructure — which is precisely why the phase order builds shared infra first and commits to the learner second.

### rationale
Five arguments, each grounded in a measurement from the profiles rather than in taste.

1. MODEL-FREE SELF-PLAY ON SPLENDOR HAS A MEASURED CEILING. chlligence ran 51,814,400 real env steps of MaskablePPO self-play with a 30-entry ELO-weighted pool on a game STRICTLY EASIER than ours (2 players, no nobles, no multi-mode, auto-discard instead of a hard cap). Their own checkpoints/pool_index.json shows generations 63-99 oscillating win_rate_vs_prev between 0.3625 and 0.6375 around 0.5 (consistent with pure noise at n=80) with ELO wandering in a ~10-point band, and several late generations measurably worse than their predecessors. That is not "needs more steps"; that is a design ceiling. We could reproduce their 52M steps in about an hour on this node, and there is no reason to believe the 53rd million is different from the 52nd.

2. SEARCH AT INFERENCE IS WORTH ~+600 ELO ON THIS GAME, MEASURED, AT FIXED WEIGHTS. BreckEmert's same-weights ablation (RL/mcts.py docstring, azero-loop branch): PUCT vs greedy with identical nets wins 63% at 25 sims, 83% at 150, 97% at 400; search@150 beat their DQN champion 29-1; their commit note says "+590 Elo at decision time." Our deployment budget is *seconds per move on a dedicated GPU* — enough for 5,000-15,000 sims. Choosing an architecture that cannot exploit test-time compute throws away the largest single Elo lever available to us. And if we are building MCTS for deployment anyway, the marginal cost of putting it in the training loop is small while the payoff (search-quality targets, no shaping) is large. That is exactly why option 3 (train PPO, search only at inference) is dominated: it pays the MCTS implementation cost without collecting the training benefit, and it fits the value function to the on-policy return of a weak policy — which inference-time search will then actively exploit off-distribution.

3. ALPHAZERO NEEDS NO REWARD SHAPING; PPO ON THIS GAME DOES. Splendor's terminal reward is ~60-140 plies away. Every profiled PPO/DQN attempt that learned anything needed elaborate hand-designed shaping: chlligence's rewardcc.py is 394 lines of board-demand vectors, phase-dependent weights, build bonuses, block/restore triggers and hoarding penalties — whose own author's assert about PBRS invariance is false (the telescoping is broken by the opponent's intervening ply), which has a farmable BUILD_BONUS attractor, and which would have to be re-derived and re-tuned for INDIVIDUAL, ONE_V_TWO (15 vs 34 thresholds) and TEAM (>30 plus a second-highest condition) separately. For a one-shot autonomous pass, a 400-line hand-tuned reward function across five win conditions is the highest-variance component we could possibly add, and it is unverifiable — no test tells you the shaping is wrong, only a plateau three days later. AlphaZero's value target is the game result. There is nothing to tune and nothing to get subtly wrong.

4. THE MULTI-PLAYER / TEAM REQUIREMENT IS SOLVED CLEANLY BY A VALUE VECTOR AND BADLY BY A SCALAR. chlligence's terminal-reward negation (self_play_env.py:236-246) is explicitly documented as depending on Phi being strictly anti-symmetric — true for 2p and symmetric 2v2, FALSE for 3p/4p free-for-all and for ONE_V_TWO. Splendor-Zero is hardcoded `Player players[2]` with `1-p` woven through four C++ files. cestpasphoto (relevance 9, MIT) already ships a working 2/3/4-player Splendor AlphaZero whose value head emits num_players tanh values, rotated by np.roll(v, next_player) on backup and np.roll(r, -curPlayer) on targets. With a per-seat vector, TEAM 2v2 is "both teammates get the same terminal entry" and ONE_V_TWO is "write the asymmetric terminal function" — no change to search, backup, or network. Critically, a teammate's turn is then automatically cooperative rather than adversarial, with no special case in the tree. There is no comparably clean way to do this with a scalar PPO advantage.

5. THE CLOSEST WORKING REFERENCE IS ALPHAZERO-SHAPED AND MIT-LICENSED. cestpasphoto's splendor/ matches OUR variant, not vanilla Splendor, on nine specific rules: token supply {2:4, 3:5, 4:7} + 5 gold; nobles = n+1 revealed, all worth 3, five 4+4-adjacent and five 3+3+3; take-2-same gated on stack >= 4 BEFORE taking; gold-on-reserve gated on `player_gems.sum() <= 9`; payment colored-then-gold; end condition evaluated when the round returns to seat 0; tiebreak points then FEWER cards; card arrays already shaped [5][8|6|4][2][7] which is exactly our 8/6/4 templates x 5 rotations; and — the one everybody else gets wrong — the 10-token cap enforced by SELECTION BLOCKING (`players_gems.sum() + gems.sum(axis=1) <= 10`), not by discard. Splendor-Zero, BreckEmert and roeey777 all implement take-then-discard, which means porting from them drags in a whole dead subsystem (2,910 of roeey777's 3,510 actions exist solely to enumerate discard combinations). Starting from the AZ-shaped MIT reference removes weeks of rules risk.

WHERE THE PPO CAMP IS RIGHT, AND WHAT I TAKE FROM IT: masking design, the paired-seed side-swapped evaluation, opponent hot-swap over persistent workers, thread-oversubscription pinning, and the fact that PPO tolerates a slow environment better. Those all survive into my plan. What does not survive is PPO as the learner.

### architecture
ONE mode-conditioned network. ~10M parameters, bf16. PyTorch.

INPUT (~1,100 float32, all in roughly [-1,1], seat-relative with the acting player at slot 0, four seat slots always present with a validity mask):

A. Board scalars (~52): bank counts per colour + gold, normalized by that mode's supply; PLUS unary/thermometer encoding of each stack 0..7 and gold 0..5 (makes the ">=4 for take-2" and "<=10 cap" predicates linearly separable — seal256's tokens_to_vector idea, written but never wired in by them).
B. 24 card slots x 27 dims = 648, fed through a WEIGHT-SHARED card encoder (27 -> 64):
   slots = 12 face-up (tier-major) + 3 own reserved + 3x3 opponent reserved.
   per card: points one-hot(6) + bonus-colour one-hot(5) + raw cost/7 (5) + RELATIVE COST max(cost - my_effective_gems, 0)/4 (5) [BreckEmert deck.py:24-27 — the best single feature in the corpus] + gold-needed-after-discounts(1) + affordable-now(1) + affordable-with-gold(1) + turns_to_buy = max(ceil(sum(missing)/3), max(missing))/8 (1) [roeey777 features.py] + present(1) + known(1).
   An opponent's DECK-reserved card is encoded as all-zero card features with known=0 plus a tier one-hot — the information-set encoding from Splendor-Zero's append_opponent_reserved_slot_raw. Board-reserved opponent cards are fully encoded (they are public via our action log).
C. Deck knowledge (~33): remaining count per (tier, template) and per (tier, colour), plus per-tier totals. Public deduction; do NOT feed a bit-packed byte as a raw magnitude (cestpasphoto's V80 does this and their own newer models skip those rows).
D. Tiles (~75): 5 slots x (5-dim requirement/4, available flag, my per-colour card shortfall, my total shortfall, each opponent's total shortfall) — includes the two-level "distance to noble" rollup from roeey777.
E. Self scalars (~90): tokens (raw + unary), permanent bonuses, points one-hot(0..20), cards owned, reserved count, per-colour buying power, log(1+buying power), distance to my own threshold.
F. Three opponent blocks x ~42 = 126 scalars: tokens, bonuses, points one-hot, cards, reserved count, is-present, IS-TEAMMATE, buying power.
G. Mode/global (~40): mode one-hot(5) {2p, 3p-ffa, 4p-ffa, 1v2, 2v2}, player-count one-hot(3), my absolute seat one-hot(4), plies-until-turn-returns-to-round-leader one-hot(4), final-round-ARMED flag, final-round-REVOCABLE flag, turn index/200, my threshold, my side's threshold, my side's aggregate score, the other side's aggregate, and for TEAM the two "second-highest individual" comparators.

BODY: concat(card embeddings 24x64=1536, non-card features ~500) -> Linear(2036, 768) -> 6 pre-LN residual blocks [LayerNorm -> Linear(768,768) -> GELU -> Linear(768,768) + skip] -> 768.

HEADS:
- Policy: 66 logits. Structured: the 30 buy/reserve logits come from a SHARED Linear(64+768 -> 1) scoring each card slot embedding conditioned on the trunk (permutation-equivariant over board slots by construction — Splendor-Zero's StructuredPolicyHead idea); the other 36 (30 gem takes, 5 CHOOSE_TILE, 1 forced-no-move) come from Linear(768,512)->GELU->Linear(512,36). Legal mask applied ADDITIVELY as -1e9 BEFORE softmax, inside the module, so exported inference can never emit an illegal move.
- Value: Linear(768,512)->GELU->Linear(512,4)->tanh. Four seat-relative outcomes; loss masked to valid seats.
- Auxiliary (weight 0.15, free supervision that matters a lot with a 100-ply horizon): (i) each seat's final points/20 (regression), (ii) game length remaining/200, (iii) predicted number of tiles each seat ends with. Aux heads are DROPPED at deployment.

Parameter count: card encoder ~6k; input projection 2036x768 = 1.56M; 6 blocks x 2 x 768^2 = 7.1M; heads ~1.0M. Total ~9.7M. Tunable 3-20M; I would not exceed 20M — Splendor is not Go, and every extra parameter costs sims/second.

Deployment footprint: ~20 MB in fp16. Irrelevant against 4 GB.

WHY NOT a transformer / entity model: cestpasphoto's V90/91/92 entity-mixer and micro-transformer variants have NO pretrained weights, no reported results, and inline "FIX:" narration. Every shipped, validated Splendor net in the corpus is a small MLP. The weight-shared card encoder plus the structured buy/reserve head gives us the one inductive bias that clearly pays (parameter sharing across 24 interchangeable card slots) for ~30 lines and near-zero risk.

### env_design
LANGUAGE / SPEED. Python + Numba `@jitclass` over ONE contiguous int16 state array with named numpy views, following cestpasphoto's design (MIT, proven for 2/3/4-player Splendor). Free functions carry `@njit(cache=True, fastmath=True, nogil=True)`; `nogil=True` is load-bearing because it lets a worker process run E concurrent games while a thread batches inference. Chosen over C++/pybind11 (Splendor-Zero) because a build system is a real one-autonomous-pass hazard, and over pure-object Python because I measured the cost of that choice in the corpus: Nikola995's dataclass env does 6,880 moves/s with 95% of the step budget spent in the legal-action mask, and roeey777's mask does an O(|legal| x 3510) linear scan over an unhashable dataclass. Accept a ~10-30s per-process jitclass warm-up (jitclasses are not cacheable) — amortized over hours.

TARGET: >=150k raw steps/s/core including the mask. Hard floor 50k/s/core.

ACTION SPACE: 66 flat, slot-indexed, never card-id-indexed.
  0-24   take 1, 2 or 3 DIFFERENT colours: C(5,1)+C(5,2)+C(5,3) = 5+10+10 = 25 (precomputed constant subsets)
  25-29  take 2 of the same colour (legal only if that stack had >= 4 before taking)
  30-41  buy face-up slot (tier*4+slot)
  42-44  buy own reserved slot
  45-56  reserve face-up slot
  57-59  reserve from deck top (tier)
  60-64  CHOOSE_TILE slot (up to n+1 = 5)
  65     forced no-move terminal marker (see below)
Note this is ~10x smaller than the ports people would naively copy (Splendor-Zero 69 with a return-gem phase, roeey777 3,510) purely because our cap is selection-blocking: there is no discard axis and no give-back phase. Delete that machinery rather than adapting it.

Buys carry NO payment in the action (Splendor-Zero's factorization) — our server pays coloured-first-then-gold deterministically, so the payment is a pure function of state. That keeps buys at 15 entries instead of thousands.

MULTI-STEP PROTOCOL -> ATOMIC MACRO-ACTIONS. SELECT_GEM x k then TAKE_GEMS_CONFIRMED, and ENTER_RESERVE then RESERVE_CARD(cardId)/RESERVE_FROM_DECK(tier), are collapsed into ONE search action each and re-expanded in the protocol adapter. This is legal because the intermediate states carry no extra information, and ENTER_RESERVE's irrevocability is satisfied by construction (an atomic action always completes). The adapter MUST resolve slot -> cardId BEFORE the action mutates the board (chlligence's get_card_for_action pattern).

CHOOSE_TILE IS A SAME-PLAYER SUB-DECISION, not a cross-product. When several tiles qualify, `apply_action` returns the SAME acting player and the mask exposes only actions 60-64 (cestpasphoto's smallworld pattern, SmallworldLogicNumba.py:238-240). Every tree edge carries a `same_player` flag; backup negates/rotates only when the turn actually changes hands (Splendor-Zero's PathStep.same_player, native_mcts.cpp:2470-2481). This avoids the x5/x6 action-space blowup that Splendor-Zero and roeey777 both pay.

HIDDEN INFORMATION — deliberately modest, because our hidden state is tiny:
  (a) Deck order: hidden. Deck COMPOSITION is public deduction and is exposed in the observation.
  (b) Opponents' DECK-reserved cards: truly hidden, at most 3 per opponent, drawn from a known unseen pool. Encoded as tier + unknown flag.
  (c) Opponents' BOARD-reserved cards: PUBLIC in our variant (visible in the action log) — store and encode them fully. This is a genuine asymmetry that every profiled repo gets wrong in one direction or the other.
Determinization: `determinize(state, rng)` samples an unseen-card assignment for (b) and a shuffle for (a). Called once per MCTS simulation, from a fixed set of K universe seeds so children are revisited consistently (cestpasphoto's PC-PIMC). This is why we need neither chance nodes nor full ISMCTS.

SPEED STRATEGY:
- Single memcpy state clone; hashable via `tobytes()`.
- Immutable card/tile tables shared by reference (frozen dataclass / static const arrays).
- Vectorized affordability for all 15 candidate cards at once: `max(cost - bonus, 0).sum(axis=1) <= gold`.
- Precomputed constant tables: the 25 gem subsets, the C(5,k) index maps, the per-mode supply vectors, action-type index ranges.
- Do NOT rebuild the full mask from scratch when a cheap incremental update suffices; but DO have a slow reference mask function retained as an oracle and diff-tested against the fast one (seal256's dual get_actions pattern).
- Reward/aux computation must not deepcopy state (chlligence's env.py:157 clone_state per step is their dominant cost).

EXPLICIT BLOCKING AMBIGUITIES TO RESOLVE FROM gameLogic.js IN PHASE 0 (do not guess):
1. Are shorter gem takes legal ALWAYS ("up to 3 different colours") or ONLY when supply/cap forces them? The brief says both. Splendor-Zero requires exactly-2-colours-remaining; cestpasphoto allows voluntary short takes. This changes the mask for ~15 of 66 actions and is worth exactly one careful read.
2. What does the server actually do when a player has no legal move? Hang, auto-skip, or end the game? Mirror it exactly. If it truly hangs, that is a product bug to flag; for training, define the terminal to match observed server behaviour and add a 1-ply self-deadlock avoidance guard in the deployed worker so we never walk into it.
3. Exact ordering within a turn: tile check vs final-round arm/disarm vs turn advance vs board refill. Especially the TEAM revocable final round.
4. Does resign exist in team/1v2 modes and how is it scored?

VALIDATION AGAINST THE NODE ENGINE (non-negotiable, five layers):
1. Bidirectional trajectory replay: 200k random Python games -> dump (initial deal, action sequence) -> replay through gameLogic.js -> assert full state equality after EVERY action. Then the reverse: drive the real Node server with random agents over the real protocol, dump, replay in Python.
2. MASK SET-EQUALITY at every replayed state. This is the check the corpus keeps missing: seal256 shipped the SAME take-2 off-by-one in both his C++ and Python engines, so his consistency tests could never catch it, and Nikola995's action-set test is literally `assert True`. A second copy of your own assumptions is not an oracle — gameLogic.js is.
3. Protocol round-trip: for every macro-action emit the exact message sequence and assert the server lands in the same state.
4. Invariant fuzzing across 1M random games: never >10 tokens; never negative supply; never >3 reserved; gold-on-reserve granted iff (gold>0 and total<10); tiles awarded exactly per rule and re-checked after every action type; final-round arm/disarm transitions match; deadlock detected identically; all five win conditions and both tiebreaks agree.
5. A golden-vector file (a few thousand states + masks + transitions) committed and run in CI, plus a format_version stamp on the observation encoding that checkpoints refuse to load across (chlligence's opponent_pool.py:174-245 gate — cheap insurance against silently mixing encodings).

CALIBRATION CHECK worth its own line: Rinascimento measured, on an IDENTICAL token economy, that the all-players-at-10-with-empty-bank deadlock is mathematically reachable at 3p (5x5+5 = 30 = 3x10) and 4p (5x7+5 = 40 = 4x10) but NOT at 2p (25 > 20), and observed stalemate rates of 1.8% / 10.9% / 9.8% under random play. Our env must reproduce that shape. If our measured rates are far off, the rules mirror is wrong — this is a free, high-signal sanity test that costs one afternoon.

### multi_mode_plan
ONE CONDITIONED NETWORK, not five specialists. Justification: the observation is padded to four seat slots with a validity mask and a per-slot teammate flag, so the input and output shapes never change with player count (this is exactly why cestpasphoto needed three separate models — their NUMBER_PLAYERS is a module constant baked into the obs shape; we fix that at the source). One training run, one arena, one deployable artifact, and transfer across modes. Modes are sampled per episode from a mixture, so every gradient step sees all of them.

MODE CONDITIONING: mode one-hot(5) + player-count one-hot(3) + absolute-seat one-hot(4) + is-teammate flags + threshold features + side-aggregate features. Crucially the seat index is a REAL feature, not something to randomize away: in ONE_V_TWO the solo is always seat 0 and always starts, so seat identity is load-bearing. Contrast with SIMPLE's selfplay_wrapper, which randomizes the seat precisely to erase seat information — correct for symmetric games, wrong for ours. We randomize which *policy instance* sits where, never the encoded seat identity.

TERMINAL VALUE VECTOR (v in [-1,1]^4, seat-relative, slot 0 = acting seat, invalid slots masked out of the loss):
- INDIVIDUAL 2/3/4p: rank-linear, v_i = 1 - 2*(rank_i - 1)/(n-1), ties share the average. Ranking by (points, then FEWER cards), exactly as the server ranks. I deliberately reject pure top/bottom rewards: cestpasphoto and SIMPLE both give +1 to the winner, -1 to the loser and EXACTLY 0 to everyone in between, which in a 4-player game leaves two of four seats with zero learning signal regardless of how close the game was.
- ONE_V_TWO: outcome computed by the server's rule (both sides check their own threshold; if both qualify, larger excess over own threshold wins; ties possible). v_solo in {+1, 0, -1}; both duo seats receive the negation identically. Duo coordination falls out of both duo seats maximizing the same entry — no communication channel, no special machinery. Add an auxiliary regression target on (own side's excess over its own threshold) to give dense gradient long before the threshold is reached; this is the mode where sparse signal bites hardest because 34 combined is a long way away.
- TEAM 2v2: v = +1/0/-1 per team, both teammates identical. Because a node maximizes its own acting seat's entry and teammates share that entry, a teammate's turn is automatically cooperative in the tree — no "paranoid vs max-n" decision to make, no sign flip to get wrong. This is the strongest single argument for the value-vector design.
- DEADLOCK (no legal move) and TURN-CAP TRUNCATION (400 plies) are DISTINCT labeled terminals. Deadlock is scored exactly as the server scores it. Truncated games are scored by current standings using the same ranking function BUT their value targets are down-weighted to 0.3 (policy targets kept at full weight). Splendor-Zero labels truncations as draws and feeds them straight into the buffer at full weight — a documented contaminant that will be much worse for us, since 3p/4p/1v2/2v2 games run longer.

TEAM CREDIT ASSIGNMENT: handled entirely by the value vector; no per-agent credit decomposition, no COMA/QMIX-style machinery. This is a deliberate simplification and I believe it is sufficient because both teammates search the same tree structure against the same terminal function.

SEARCH-SIDE MULTIPLAYER: backup the full 4-vector, rotated at each edge so index 0 is that node's acting seat. PUCT at a node uses Q = child_value_vector[0] after rotation. This is max-n backup. Explicitly FIX cestpasphoto's MCTS.py:71-72 approximation `q = [q0 if seat==0 else -q0/(n-1)]`, which assumes symmetric evenly-split opposition — already crude for 3-4p free-for-all and outright wrong for teams and for asymmetric thresholds. Use the actual per-seat entries.

MODE MIXTURE CURRICULUM:
- Games 0 - ~300k: 100% 2-player. Fastest signal, cleanest structure, no deadlock (mathematically impossible at 2p), and it is the mode with reference points in the literature so we can tell whether we are on track.
- Games ~300k - ~1M: ramp to 2p 40% / 3p-ffa 15% / 4p-ffa 15% / 1v2 15% / 2v2 15%.
- Steady state: 2p 25% / 3p-ffa 15% / 4p-ffa 15% / 1v2 20% / 2v2 25%. The two non-individual modes get over-weighted because their terminal logic is unique and unshared, so they need the most mode-specific data; the free-for-alls share most structure with 2p.

SPECIALIZATION FALLBACK (measured, not assumed): after Phase 4, fine-tune the shared trunk on each single mode for ~3 GPU-hours and run a 400-game paired arena per mode. Ship a per-mode checkpoint only where the fine-tune beats the shared net by >40 Elo with non-overlapping 95% CIs. Since a checkpoint is ~20 MB, "one artifact plus up to four small deltas" is still trivially deployable and keeps the one-artifact preference honest without betting the whole run on it.

LEAGUE / OPPONENT DIVERSITY FOR MULTI-SEAT MODES: 90% of games are pure self-play (all seats current weights). 10% are mixed: 1-2 seats drawn from a bounded historical pool with PERMANENTLY PINNED anchors (never evicted), and in 2v2 sometimes a current-net player is paired with a HISTORICAL teammate — this is the only cheap test of whether the duo/team coordination is robust or is a fragile self-play convention. Only current-net seats are recorded. Pool eviction must NOT be "drop the lowest Elo" (chlligence's opponent_pool.py:87), which under a monotonically drifting chain degenerates into "keep the last 30 generations" and discards exactly the stylistically different opponents that guard against cycling — a plausible mechanical cause of their plateau. Use reservoir sampling plus pinned anchors.

### training_plan_4xA100
PARALLELISM LAYOUT (and an explicit disagreement with the brief's framing):

DO NOT USE DDP. For a ~10M-parameter MLP the learner is roughly 100x oversized on ONE A100: a batch-4096 forward+backward is ~6*10e6*4096 = 2.5e11 FLOPs ~= 2 ms at ~125 TFLOP/s bf16, i.e. ~400 optimizer steps/s = ~1.6M samples/s of training throughput, against a self-play production rate of ~1,250 recorded positions/s. DDP would add process groups, NCCL init, launcher complexity, sharded checkpointing and silent-hang failure modes for exactly zero throughput. Revisit only above ~100M parameters.

Layout on the single node:
- GPU 0: LEARNER. Single process, bf16 autocast, no DDP.
- GPUs 1-3: three INFERENCE SERVERS, one process each. Each holds a request queue, coalesces for up to 1 ms or until 1,024 leaves, runs `torch.inference_mode()` bf16, returns (policy logits, value vector). Weights refreshed from the learner every ~120 s via a versioned file + atomic swap; the file carries the observation format_version and the server REFUSES a mismatch loudly.
- CPU: 120 actor worker processes (leave ~8 cores for learner, dataloader, inference servers, OS). Each worker runs E=16 concurrent games with an interleaved MCTS scheduler so it always has ~16 pending leaves to batch to a server over shared memory. Total concurrent games ~1,920.
- MANDATORY ops detail: set OMP_NUM_THREADS = MKL_NUM_THREADS = OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS = 1 via os.environ.setdefault BEFORE any other import, and call torch.set_num_threads(1) inside each worker init. chlligence's train.py:19-25 states the arithmetic (20 workers x 24 cores = 480 threads on 24 cores, ~2x slowdown); on a 128-core node this failure mode is worse, not better, and it is the single most common way a "correct" pipeline runs at 20% of its speed with no error message.

SEARCH BUDGETS (Playout Cap Randomization, KataGo):
- Full search: 600 sims, Dirichlet(alpha = 10/num_legal, eps 0.25) at the root, forced playouts n_forced = floor(sqrt(k*P*sum_N)) with k=2, policy-target pruning applied before export. 25% of moves. RECORDED.
- Fast search: 120 sims, no noise, no forced playouts. 75% of moves. NOT recorded.
- Expected 0.25*600 + 0.75*120 = 240 sims per move.
- Temperature 1.0 (sample from visits) for the first 12 plies, argmax after. K=6 determinization universes.

THROUGHPUT ARITHMETIC (conservative):
- Numba rules step + mask: ~3 us. Python tree select/expand/backup: ~40-100 us. Budget 8,000 sims/s/core (a deliberately conservative number; if the tree loop lands nearer 40 us it is ~20k).
- 120 cores x 8,000 = ~1.0M MCTS simulations/s aggregate.
- / 240 sims per move = ~4,200 agent moves/s.
- Average game ~100 plies (2p ~60-80, 4p ~140, per Rinascimento's ~141 ticks and chlligence's observed lengths) => ~42 self-play games/s = ~150k games/hour = ~3.6M games/day.
- Recorded positions: 4,200 * 0.25 = ~1,050/s = ~91M/day.
- GPU load: 1.0M leaf evals/s across 3 GPUs = ~333k/s each. At batch 1,024 and 10M params that is ~2*10e6*1024 = 2e10 FLOPs/batch = ~0.2 ms, i.e. ~325 batches/s needed against a ceiling of several thousand. GPUs are ~10-20% utilized. THE SYSTEM IS CPU-BOUND, WHICH IS THE CORRECT PLACE TO BE — it means we can raise sims/move or net size almost for free later.

LEARNER:
- Batch 4,096. AdamW, lr 2e-4 with 2,000-step linear warmup then cosine to 2e-5 over the run, weight_decay 1e-4 on non-norm params, grad clip 1.0, bf16 autocast.
- Loss = CE(pruned visit distribution, masked log-softmax policy) + 1.0 * masked-MSE(value vector, blended z) + 0.15 * aux losses.
- Value target z = 0.5*(root Q of the most-visited child) + 0.5*(terminal outcome vector) — Splendor-Zero's blended target, one line, large variance reduction at a 100-ply horizon.
- Throttled to a target SAMPLE REUSE of 4-8x: at 1,050 positions/s and 6x reuse that is ~6,300 samples/s = ~1.5 steps/s at batch 4,096. The learner is idle >99% of the time; this is fine and intentional. Do not "use up" the spare capacity by inflating the net until Phase 4 tells you the current net is the bottleneck.

REPLAY BUFFER — one non-obvious recommendation that pays for itself: store the COMPACT GAME STATE (int16 array, ~300-600 bytes) plus a SPARSE policy target (top-k indices + probs) plus the 4-vector and sample weight; re-encode the ~1,100-dim observation in dataloader workers on the fly (encoding is a cheap numba call). 20M positions x ~800 B = ~16 GB RAM instead of ~52 GB for dense fp16 observations. The real win is that you can CHANGE THE OBSERVATION ENCODING WITHOUT DISCARDING THE BUFFER — which is exactly the disaster chlligence's format_version gate exists to detect after the fact. Rolling window over the last N generations, trimmed by generation (not by individual sample), ramping 4 -> 20 generations as training proceeds (Splendor-Zero's _rolling_replay_window_generations). Per-sample zlib level 1 if RAM gets tight (cestpasphoto Coach.py:84).

DATA AUGMENTATION (free ~4-12x samples, zero simulation cost): permute the 4 face-up cards within each tier and apply the SAME permutation to the policy target and the mask at BOTH the buy indices and the reserve indices (cestpasphoto get_symmetries, SplendorLogicNumba.py:255-301); separately permute the acting player's own reserved slots. Do NOT permute colours unless the tile requirements are colour-symmetric under that permutation — our tiles are cyclic (4+4 adjacent, 3+3+3 consecutive), so ONLY the 5 cyclic rotations and the reflection are valid colour symmetries; that is a genuine extra 10x if the card templates are also cyclic, which the brief says they are. Verify the symmetry group against gameLogic.js before enabling colour augmentation — a wrong symmetry silently teaches the net a false invariant.

WALL-CLOCK SCHEDULE (5-day PBS job, checkpoint every 10 min, resumable, idempotent self-restart):
- h0-h1: warm-up at reduced search (150 full / 40 fast) to get the net off random quickly; 100% 2p.
- h1-h6: full search budgets, 100% 2p ramping to the mixture. ~800k games by h6.
- h6-h24: full mixture. ~2.7M more games. Expect the first genuinely strong 2p checkpoint here.
- h24-h72: steady state. ~7M more games. Elo vs the FIXED anchor set should still be climbing.
- h72-h120: diminishing returns; raise full-search sims to 1,000 and lower the learning rate. Ship whichever checkpoint wins the final arena.
Total ~12-15M self-play games. For calibration: cestpasphoto's shipped models came from ~50-80k games and Splendor-Zero from perhaps 10^5-10^6; we exceed the entire published corpus within the first day. Expect the day-3 checkpoint to be what ships.

EVALUATION BUDGET: reserve ~10% of node time for the arena. Paired-seed, seat-rotated matches (both games of a pair use the SAME deck seed so only the seating differs — chlligence evaluate.py:55-68; this cancels deal variance rather than adding an independent noisy sample). For 3p/4p rotate through a Latin square of seat assignments, NOT cestpasphoto's biased 1-vs-(n-1) arrangement. n >= 400 paired games per pairing per mode. Fit a joint Bradley-Terry / BayesElo model over the whole result matrix — do NOT chain pairwise deltas the way seal256 and cestpasphoto do. Report per-mode Elo with 95% CIs, plus wins-as-seat-k broken out as a standing seat-asymmetry detector, plus a separate STALE/deadlock bucket (Rinascimento's WinStaleLostStatsFull).

FIXED ANCHORS THAT ARE NEVER EVICTED: uniform-random-legal; the heuristic bot; heuristic-value MCTS at 200 sims; and milestone checkpoints (gen 5, 10, 20, 40, 80). chlligence's rating is a self-referential K-factor chain anchored on an arbitrary 1200 with no fixed reference player anywhere in the system — 1232 means nothing, and their 32-point "rise" is drift. Without external anchors we cannot distinguish progress from noise, and that is how a 52M-step run ends with no usable strength number.

### inference_plan_3060
SEARCH AT INFERENCE — yes, and generously. This is where the largest measured Elo gain in the entire corpus lives (+590 Elo, same weights, BreckEmert).

Runtime: a Python worker holding an outbound websocket to the game server.
- Model: fp16, ~20 MB. Peak VRAM including activations at batch 64 and CUDA context: <400 MB of 4 GB. Non-issue. Use plain eager PyTorch; adopt torch.compile or CUDA graphs ONLY if they prove stable in the soak test — a compile-time stall inside a 3-minute clock is a worse failure than 20% slower inference.
- Batched leaf evaluation with VIRTUAL LOSS: descend 32-64 paths applying (N+1, W-1) so in-batch siblings diverge, evaluate all pending leaves in one forward, then roll the virtual loss back exactly and apply the real backup. BreckEmert measured 0.75 s/move -> 0.18 s/move at batch 8 -> 0.13 s at batch 16 for 150 sims on CPU; on a 3060 with batch 64 this is far cheaper. Assert that batch=1 reproduces the sequential algorithm bit-for-bit as a unit test.
- Determinization at inference: K=16-32 universes, RE-SAMPLED PER SIMULATION exactly as in training (consistency between train and deploy matters more than a marginally better estimator). Add Splendor-Zero's anti-clairvoyance penalty: a small random PUCT penalty drawn uniformly from [0, 0.02/0.06/0.12] for tier 1/2/3 on RESERVE_FROM_DECK actions at the root, to offset the determinizer's false certainty about the deck top. Optionally a 2-of-3 consensus vote on the final move if the budget allows (BreckEmert's early-exit trick: run two searches, return immediately if they agree, else run a third).

TIME BUDGET against the 3min+10s clock: target 1.5 s of search per move, hard cap 2.5 s, 0.5 s reserved for network round-trip and protocol emission. At an estimated 3,000-10,000 sims/s on the deployment host (Python tree ops dominate; GPU is ~10% of the budget) that is roughly 5,000-15,000 simulations per move — an order of magnitude MORE search than training-time full searches (600). Expect the deployed agent to be meaningfully stronger than the training policy, per BreckEmert's measured curve (63% @ 25 sims, 83% @ 150, 97% @ 400 vs greedy, same weights).

MANDATORY SAFETY BELTS (each one addresses a specific failure I found in the corpus):
1. ALWAYS-READY FALLBACK: from t = 50 ms the worker holds the policy-argmax-over-mask move in hand. A search stall, an exception, or a clock warning immediately submits it. No timeouts, ever.
2. RE-VALIDATE BEFORE SENDING: recompute the legal mask from the freshly received server state and assert the chosen action is still legal before emitting. Never trust the search's internal state as authoritative.
3. HYDRATION WITH CONSISTENCY VALIDATION: reconstruct engine state from the server payload and RE-DERIVE bonuses and points from purchased cards, rejecting the payload if the derived values disagree (Splendor-Zero's validate_public_state_consistency, py_splendor.cpp:255 — the single most reusable live-play pattern in the corpus). Unlisted deck cards are auto-filled from the catalog complement.
4. SHADOW-STATE HIDDEN-INFO TRACKER: maintain hidden_reserved_tier[(seat, slot)] by diffing consecutive observations — when a slot goes empty -> hidden, infer the tier from which deck count dropped or which face-up row changed, and forget it the moment the card is revealed (Splendor-Zero spendee/shadow_state.py). Board-reserved cards are recorded as KNOWN from the action log.
5. SELF-DEADLOCK GUARD: a 1-ply check that refuses any action which provably leaves us at 10 tokens with 3 reserved and nothing affordable next turn, unless every action does. Our server has no pass; walking into that state is a game-breaking outcome, not merely a bad one.
6. MASK-NONEMPTY ASSERT every single decision; log and alert rather than softmaxing over an all -1e9 logit vector (which silently degenerates to uniform-over-illegal — Splendor-Zero and cestpasphoto both have this latent crash, guarded only by an always-legal pass action we do not have).
7. Reconnection, idempotent action submission, and a per-move structured log (chosen action, visits, root value vector, sims actually evaluated) for post-hoc debugging. Track search_slots_evaluated separately from the nominal budget — Splendor-Zero silently drops simulations to pending-eval/cycle collisions and their published "250,000 simulations" is a requested budget, not an evaluated one.

Mode handling at deployment: the single conditioned checkpoint (plus any per-mode fine-tune deltas from Phase 5) is selected by the mode field in the server's game state. Weights are ~20 MB each, so shipping all five is free.

### risks
- RULES DRIFT from server/gameLogic.js — the top risk by a wide margin, and the one that silently wastes the whole run. Highest-danger areas: the TEAM revocable final round (state, not a latch), CHOOSE_TILE timing and the multi-qualifier choice, the exact intra-turn ordering of tile-check vs final-round arm/disarm vs turn advance vs board refill, and whether short gem takes are always legal or only when forced. MITIGATION: bidirectional 200k-game trajectory replay, MASK SET-EQUALITY at every state (not just transition equality), protocol round-trip, invariant fuzzing, golden vectors in CI. GO/NO-GO gate before any training.
- DEADLOCK SEMANTICS WRONG. Our token economy makes the all-at-10-empty-bank state mathematically reachable at 3p and 4p and Rinascimento measured ~10% of random games hitting it. Our server has NO pass action. If the server hangs there, a trained agent that steers into it breaks live games; if our env models it differently from the server, every 3p/4p value target is subtly wrong. MITIGATION: resolve the server's actual behaviour in Phase 0, mirror it exactly, add a labeled terminal, add a 1-ply self-deadlock guard in the deployed worker, and monitor the rate in production.
- PYTHON MCTS TOO SLOW, starving self-play. MITIGATION: measured in Phase 2 against an explicit floor (>=5k sims/s/core). Escalation ladder: (a) cut full-search sims 600 -> 300, (b) raise games-per-worker to improve batching, (c) promote the select/backup loop into numba with fixed-size arrays, (d) fall back to the PPO learner on the identical env. Note the plan already has ~10x GPU headroom, so a 3x CPU shortfall is survivable.
- STRATEGY FUSION / OVER-CONFIDENCE about the deck top from determinization, making RESERVE_FROM_DECK look better than it is. MITIGATION: the anti-clairvoyance PUCT penalty; plus a standing diagnostic comparing deck-reserve frequency against a no-hidden-info ablation. If the agent deck-reserves far more than a perfect-information ablation, the penalty is too weak.
- MULTI-SEAT BACKUP / ROTATION SIGN ERRORS — silent, and they produce a plausibly-training-but-weak agent. This is the classic AlphaZero-for-N-players bug and cestpasphoto ships a wrong version of it (the -q/(n-1) approximation). MITIGATION: the Phase-2 MCTS-only gate (search must beat the heuristic before any net exists, which isolates search bugs from learning bugs); unit tests asserting the value vector is anti-symmetric in a symmetric 2p position and permutation-consistent under seat relabeling; the seat-asymmetry detector in every arena run.
- THE MODE-CONDITIONED NET UNDER-FITS ONE MODE, most likely ONE_V_TWO (most different dynamics, asymmetric thresholds, fixed starting seat). MITIGATION: per-mode Elo tracked separately from day one, and the Phase-5 measured fine-tune fallback (~3 GPU-hours per mode, ship only on a >40 Elo win with disjoint CIs).
- DUO / TEAM COORDINATION IS A FRAGILE SELF-PLAY CONVENTION that does not survive a different partner. No profiled work has any evidence here. MITIGATION: the 10% mixed games specifically include current-net paired with a HISTORICAL teammate; report that win rate separately as a coordination-robustness metric.
- SELF-REFERENTIAL ELO with no external anchor — how chlligence ended a 52M-step run unable to say whether it had improved. MITIGATION: permanently pinned anchors (random, heuristic, heuristic-MCTS, milestone checkpoints), a joint BayesElo fit over the whole matrix, per-mode CIs, and a hard rule that no promotion decision is made on <400 paired games.
- TRUNCATION CONTAMINATION of value targets — games hitting the ply cap scored as draws or as full-weight outcomes. Worse for us than for 2p projects because 3p/4p/1v2/2v2 run longer. MITIGATION: distinct terminal class, value-target weight 0.3, policy targets kept, and the truncation rate reported per mode (if it exceeds ~2%, raise the cap or investigate a stalling attractor).
- MASK BUGS: an all-zero mask softmaxing to uniform-over-illegal (latent in Splendor-Zero and cestpasphoto, guarded only by a pass action we do not have), or centering/normalizing over illegal entries (BreckEmert's dueling advantage mean spans all 141 actions including illegal ones). MITIGATION: assert mask.any() every decision; additive -1e9 BEFORE softmax and before any centering; mask set-equality against gameLogic.js.
- LICENSING. chlligence, Splendor-Zero, seal256, BreckEmert and Nikola995 have NO license file (all-rights-reserved by default; Splendor-Zero's README MIT claim is unexecuted). SIMPLE is GPL-3.0 (its README's MIT badge is wrong) — copying from it would impose copyleft on our trainer. MITIGATION: copy code only from cestpasphoto, Rinascimento and roeey777 (all MIT, with attribution and notices retained); re-implement everything else from the described design.
- HPC / OPERATIONAL FRAGILITY on the PBS-scheduled node: wall-clock limits, node preemption, a 16 GB replay buffer that must survive a restart, and 120 worker processes that must not silently die. MITIGATION: checkpoint model + optimizer + replay buffer + RNG state every 10 minutes to local scratch; idempotent resumable restart; a supervisor that respawns dead workers and alerts if actor throughput drops >30% from the rolling median; log samples/s, sims/s, games/s and per-mode counts continuously.

Keep Gumbel as a Phase-5 experiment only if the low-sim regime disappoints.

GO/NO-GO: heuristic beats random >=90% in every mode; match runner sustains >=2,000 games/min aggregate; Elo fit reproducible across seeds; baseline seat advantages measured and recorded (in ONE_V_TWO seat 0 is structurally special — quantify it now so later anomalies are interpretable).
- PHASE 2 — SEARCH WITH NO LEARNING. PUCT MCTS with a uniform prior and the heuristic as the leaf evaluator, determinized per simulation, with the same_player edge flag, the per-seat value vector, and every mode's terminal function wired in. GO/NO-GO: MCTS@400 beats the heuristic bot >=75% in 2p and >=60% in 3p/4p/1v2/2v2 against the relevant chance baseline; >=5,000 sims/s/core (floor 3,000); batch=1 reproduces sequential search bit-for-bit; the anti-symmetry and seat-permutation unit tests pass. THIS PHASE IS THE MOST IMPORTANT DE-RISKING STEP IN THE PLAN: it validates search, determinization, multi-seat backup and all five terminal functions BEFORE a single gradient step, so any later failure is isolable to learning. seal256's plain MCTS was +750 Elo over random and Rinascimento's tuned search beat random 96-99%, so this bar is achievable.
- PHASE 3 — ALPHAZERO LOOP, 2-PLAYER ONLY, SMALL SCALE. Full pipeline end to end but deliberately small: 16 workers, 1 inference GPU, 1 learner GPU, width-512 4-block net (~4M params), 300 full / 60 fast sims. Verify the loop closes: buffer fills, weights refresh, format_version gate fires on a deliberate mismatch, checkpoint/resume works. GO/NO-GO at 12 hours (~200k games): the candidate beats the heuristic bot >=80%; beats Phase-2 heuristic-MCTS@400 >=60% at EQUAL search budget; policy top-1 agreement with search visits >45%; value explained-variance >0.3 and rising; policy-loss entropy floor diagnostic shows real improvement rather than approach to the target noise floor. If this does not clear, DO NOT SCALE — debug, or invoke the PPO fallback on the same infrastructure.
- PHASE 4 — MULTI-MODE AT FULL SCALE. Switch to the mode-conditioned ~10M net, 120 workers, 3 inference GPUs, 600/120 sims, the mode-mixture curriculum, augmentation on (with the colour-symmetry group verified against gameLogic.js), the 10% mixed-opponent games with pinned anchors, and the 10% arena budget. GO/NO-GO at 24h and 48h: Elo against the FIXED anchor set (never against the previous generation) climbing >=+150 per 24h in the first two days; every mode individually >=+300 Elo over the heuristic anchor by h48; no mode regressing more than 50 Elo over any 12h window; truncation rate <2% per mode; deadlock rate in self-play not drifting upward (an upward drift means the agent is learning to force deadlocks, which our terminal definition may be mispricing).
- PHASE 5 — SPECIALIZATION CHECK + DEPLOYMENT WORKER. Fine-tune the shared trunk per mode for ~3 GPU-hours each and run 400 paired games per mode; ship a specialist only where it beats the shared net by >40 Elo with disjoint 95% CIs. Build the deployment worker: websocket client, hydration with consistency validation, shadow-state hidden-info tracker, wall-clock-budgeted batched search with virtual loss, always-ready fallback move, re-validate-before-send, self-deadlock guard, reconnection, structured per-move logging. GO/NO-GO: 500 games against the frozen champion THROUGH THE REAL NODE SERVER with zero protocol errors, zero timeouts, zero illegal actions; reconstructed-state agreement with the server 100%; p99 move latency <2.5s on the RTX 3060; peak VRAM <1 GB; the same-weights search-vs-greedy ablation reproduces a large positive gap (expect >=+300 Elo).
- PHASE 6 — HARDENING AND HANDOVER. 48-hour soak of the deployment worker under induced network faults and clock pressure; final full arena across all modes and all anchors with a published Elo table and CIs; freeze the observation format_version; package the artifact (weights + encoder + action table + protocol adapter + a one-command inference entry point); write the strength report honestly, separating measured claims from extrapolations.

### expected_strength
Calibrated, with the uncertainty stated rather than smoothed over.

REFERENCE POINTS. No public Splendor agent has a credible superhuman claim. The best-evidenced is Splendor-Zero: rank 1 on spendee.mattle.online at a 2420 site rating, 80% over 125 two-player games — but that is one screenshot, n=125 (Wilson 95% roughly [72%, 86%]), against a field the bot itself filtered by rating via --min-opponent-rating and _kick_low_rated_players. It was produced by an AlphaZero + IS-MCTS system with a ~0.8M-parameter MLP, trained CPU-only on a 44-core node, deploying at very large simulation counts. cestpasphoto's 2p agent (10/10 vs Lapidary AI, n=10, meaningless CI) came from perhaps 50-80k self-play games on ONE core. chlligence's 52M-step PPO agent has no measured strength at all and had plateaued.

Our design is Splendor-Zero's class of system with roughly 100x its training compute, a correct multiplayer value representation it does not have, a ~12x larger network, and a comparable-or-larger inference search budget.

WHAT I EXPECT:
- 2-PLAYER: strong-amateur to expert human level; comfortably the strongest agent in the profiled corpus. Against our heuristic anchor I expect +700 to +1000 Elo. High confidence, because 2p is where all the reference points are and where the structure is simplest (and, usefully, deadlock-free).
- 3p and 4p INDIVIDUAL: clearly the best available agent for these modes — there is essentially no competition, since Splendor-Zero, chlligence, BreckEmert and seal256 are all hardcoded 2-player and cestpasphoto's 3p/4p models were trained at 400 sims with 1 epoch and evaluated under a biased 1-vs-(n-1) protocol. Confidence moderate. Multiplayer free-for-all has irreducible outcome variance (kingmaking, three-way tempo races), so Elo gaps compress and win rates against strong opponents will look less dominant than in 2p even if the play is better.
- ONE_V_TWO: confident it plays the solo seat well (it is a single-agent optimization against a known joint threshold). Less confident on the duo, where two independently-searching agents must coordinate toward a combined-34 threshold with no communication channel. Self-play will find A coordination convention; whether it survives a different partner is exactly what the mixed-teammate diagnostic measures. Plan on this being the weakest mode.
- TEAM 2v2: similar, with the added wrinkle that the revocable final round creates a genuinely subtle endgame (a team can arm and then disarm the trigger). If the rules mirror is exact, search handles this well; if it is not, this is where the error will show up first and most expensively.

DEPLOYMENT PREMIUM: the shipped agent will be measurably stronger than the trained policy, because deployment search (5,000-15,000 sims) is an order of magnitude above training-time full searches (600). BreckEmert's same-weights curve (63% at 25 sims, 83% at 150, 97% at 400 vs greedy) implies +300 to +600 Elo from this alone. I regard this as the most reliable single component of the expected strength.

WHAT I WILL NOT PROMISE: "superhuman." Nobody in this corpus has demonstrated it, the measurement infrastructure to establish it (a rated human population playing our specific variant) does not exist, and the modes we care about most have no human baseline whatsoever. What I will commit to is a falsifiable claim: a published per-mode Elo table against a fixed, reproducible anchor ladder, with 95% CIs and >=400 paired games per pairing, plus the same-weights search ablation isolating how much of the strength comes from search. That is a stronger deliverable than any strength adjective, and it is what every project in the corpus failed to produce.

### disagreements_with_naive_choice
I disagree with plain MaskablePPO self-play on eleven specific points. Ordered by how much each one costs.

1. THE PLATEAU IS MEASURED, ON AN EASIER GAME. chlligence's own checkpoints record 51.8M real steps of MaskablePPO with a 30-entry ELO-weighted pool on 2-player, no-nobles, auto-discard Splendor, ending with generation-vs-previous win rates oscillating 0.3625-0.6375 around 0.5 and ELO drifting inside a ~10-point band for the final 35 generations, with several late generations worse than their predecessors. We can reproduce their entire run in about an hour on this node. There is no evidence that step 53,000,000 differs from step 52,000,000, and the game we need to solve is strictly harder (nobles with a choice action, five colours' worth of hidden reserves, three win regimes, four seats).

2. PPO THROWS AWAY THE BIGGEST MEASURED ELO LEVER. Our deployment budget is seconds per move on a dedicated GPU; a pure policy net uses about a millisecond of it. BreckEmert measured +590 Elo from search alone at fixed weights, and 97% vs greedy at 400 sims. If we are going to build MCTS for deployment regardless, refusing to use it during training is paying the full implementation cost for half the benefit — which is precisely why I also reject option 3 (the pure hybrid), not just option 1.

3. THE HYBRID HAS A SPECIFIC TECHNICAL FLAW, not just an efficiency one. A PPO critic is fitted to the on-policy return of a weak policy. Inference-time search will drive the position off that distribution and then exploit the critic's errors there. AlphaZero's value is trained on search-generated data, so it is accurate exactly where search goes. The hybrid is a reasonable rescue plan; it is not a good primary.

4. "SIMPLER" IS BACKWARDS ONCE YOU COUNT THE REWARD FUNCTION. PPO on a 60-140-ply sparse-reward game needs shaping. chlligence's rewardcc.py is 394 lines of demand vectors, phase-dependent weights, build bonuses, block/restore triggers, hoarding penalties and step penalties — with a false PBRS-invariance assert, a farmable BUILD_BONUS attractor, and a terminal early-return that skips the shaping on the single most important transition in the episode. And all of that would have to be re-derived and re-tuned for INDIVIDUAL, ONE_V_TWO (15 vs 34) and TEAM (>30 plus a second-highest condition) separately, with no test that can tell you it is wrong. AlphaZero's value target is the game result; there is nothing to tune. For a one-shot autonomous pass, "no reward function to design" is a larger risk reduction than "no MCTS to write."

5. THE SCALAR-VALUE / ZERO-SUM FRAMING BREAKS AT N>2 AND FOR TEAMS. chlligence's own code documents that its terminal-reward negation requires Phi to be strictly anti-symmetric — true for 2p and symmetric 2v2, false for 3p/4p free-for-all and for ONE_V_TWO. A per-seat value vector makes teams cooperative by construction (teammates share an entry, so a teammate's node maximizes the same quantity) and makes asymmetric thresholds a one-function change. There is no equally clean scalar formulation.

6. DO NOT USE DDP — this is a disagreement with the hardware framing, not just the algorithm. A 10M-parameter MLP learner needs ~1.5-4 optimizer steps/s to keep up with self-play, against a single-A100 capacity of ~400. DDP would contribute process groups, NCCL init, sharded checkpoints and silent-hang modes for zero throughput. Use 1 learner GPU + 3 batched inference GPUs. Revisit only above ~100M parameters.

7. DO NOT USE SB3 / sb3-contrib. MaskablePPO is single-process, single-GPU, has no DDP, and its opponent-inside-the-env pattern forces one single-sample CPU forward per opponent ply per worker — chlligence's exact bottleneck, with 22 workers each doing 1.58M-param CPU forwards on one thread. On this node that pattern leaves three A100s idle. A ~600-line custom loop is both faster and less mysterious.

8. DO NOT PORT THE DISCARD MACHINERY. Our 10-token cap is selection-blocking with no discard. That deletes an entire subsystem others carry: 2,910 of roeey777's 3,510 actions exist only to enumerate discard combinations, Splendor-Zero has a whole return-gem sub-turn phase with its own state flag, BreckEmert factors a discard-count axis into 95 of his 141 actions, and chlligence's HOARDING_PENALTY exists solely to paper over auto-discard. Our action space is ~66 because of this, not despite it. Capacity belongs in the MASK, and the shorter forced takes belong there too.

9. DO NOT ADD AN ALWAYS-LEGAL PASS ACTION. Every profiled engine has one, usually as a mask-nonempty guarantee (chlligence action_mask.py:91 sets mask[50]=True unconditionally). Our server has no pass, and Rinascimento's arithmetic on our identical token economy shows the no-legal-move state is genuinely reachable at 3p and 4p at ~10% under random play. Papering over it with an invented pass would train an agent that walks into a state the server cannot process. Handle it as a labeled terminal that mirrors the server, and guard it at deployment.

10. DO NOT REPORT SELF-REFERENTIAL ELO. chlligence's rating is a K-factor chain of each generation against the previous one, anchored on an arbitrary 1200 with no fixed reference player anywhere in the system. 1232 is not a strength measurement and the 32-point "rise" is drift. Build the heuristic anchor FIRST, pin it permanently, and fit a joint BayesElo over the whole result matrix — not chained pairwise deltas (seal256 and cestpasphoto both chain, and both report numbers that cannot be composed).

11. I ALSO DISAGREE WITH THE OPPOSITE OVERSHOOT — full ISMCTS. Our hidden state is at most three cards per opponent drawn from a publicly known unseen pool, plus deck order. Splendor-Zero built a true single-observer IS-MCTS with information-set hashing, cycle guards and dropped simulations, and its author's own bridge silently falls back to plain MCTS when it fails to make progress. Per-simulation determinization (PIMC) with a small fixed universe set, plus an anti-clairvoyance penalty on deck-reserve, gets nearly all of the benefit for a fraction of the surface area. Likewise I reject explicit chance nodes: fold refills into the determinization and the whole class of chance-backup bugs (seal256 divides the backed-up reward by the number of chance children, which quietly divides the value of every purchase line by ~30) simply cannot occur.

WHERE THE PPO CAMP IS RIGHT, AND WHERE I WOULD SWITCH: masking design, paired-seed evaluation, opponent hot-swap over persistent workers, and thread pinning all survive into my plan unchanged. And PPO genuinely tolerates a slow environment better. If Phase 2 shows we cannot afford useful simulation counts, the correct move is to swap the learner to MaskablePPO on the identical env/observation/action/mask/eval stack and keep MCTS at inference — which is exactly why the phase order builds the shared infrastructure first and commits to the learning algorithm second. That is not a hedge; it is the reason this plan has a high probability of producing SOMETHING strong from one pass regardless of which learner wins.

### lens
MAXIMIZE FINAL PLAYING STRENGTH on 4xA100-40GB + one RTX 3060 for deployment, accepting well-understood engineering complexity. I treat "strength" as measured against fixed, non-drifting anchors (uniform-random-legal, a 1-ply greedy bot, and a fixed 2000-sim NN-free MCTS), not against self-referential generation-over-generation win rates — because every profiled project except Splendor-Zero measured itself against a moving target and mistook drift for progress. Under this lens the decisive question is not "which algorithm is easiest to get running" but "which algorithm converts 4xA100 of FLOPs into better decisions", and for this specific variant the answer is unambiguous.

### recommended_algorithm
**Multiplayer AlphaZero with search in the training loop — specifically: Gumbel-AlphaZero (sequential halving at the root, n=32-64 simulations) over a PUCT tree, with per-seat value VECTORS and max^n backup, PIMC root re-determinization on a shared action-path-keyed tree, one mode-and-role-conditioned network trained on a mixture of all five configurations, C5 colour-symmetry augmentation, and PUCT search retained at deployment with an exact final-round solver.**

Concretely, the stack is:

- **Target producer**: Gumbel AlphaZero. Sample m=16 root actions without replacement via Gumbel top-k, allocate n=48 simulations by sequential halving, form the improved policy from completed Q-values. Policy target = improved-policy distribution (not raw visit counts); value target = 0.5*(root value estimate) + 0.5*(game outcome), KataGo-style blend.
- **Why Gumbel over classic PUCT+Dirichlet**: it gives a *provable* policy improvement at low simulation counts, and it deletes three of the four hyperparameters that most commonly cause a first-attempt AlphaZero run to silently fail (Dirichlet alpha, noise epsilon, temperature schedule). Our median legal-action count is 8, so m=16 usually covers the ENTIRE legal set — the root becomes near-exhaustive and the improvement operator is essentially exact. Classic PUCT is implemented as a flag and A/B'd in Phase 5; whichever wins the arena ships.
- **Playout Cap Randomization** (KataGo / cestpasphoto / Splendor-Zero all use it): 25% of moves get the full n=48 search WITH Gumbel root exploration and are the ONLY moves written to the replay buffer; 75% get n=12 with argmax and merely advance the game. ~3x more games per unit compute at identical target quality.
- **Chance and hidden information**: no explicit chance nodes. Re-determinize the entire hidden state (deck orders + opponents' deck-reserved identities) once per simulation from the publicly-known unseen-card multiset, cycling K=16 seeded "universes", on a SHARED tree keyed by action path from the root. Averaging over universes at shared parents is the correct expectation; explicit chance branching (~30-way on every refill) would annihilate search depth.
- **Network input is the information set only.** Never the true deck. This is non-negotiable and is what makes the visit distribution a valid information-set policy target.
- **MaskablePPO is retained but demoted**: it is (a) an optional bootstrap prior, (b) a permanent frozen arena baseline, and (c) the pre-built fallback if the AZ loop fails its Phase-5 gate. The actor/learner/arena infrastructure is written so that PPO and AZ differ ONLY in the "target producer" module.

### rationale
**1. The strength evidence in the profiles points one way, and it is not close.**
Across ten profiled projects there is exactly one externally-validated strong Splendor agent: inhabae/Splendor-Zero (AlphaZero + IS-MCTS), rank 1 on Spendee. And there is exactly one clean, internally-controlled *ablation* of search: BreckEmert measured 150-sim PUCT beating his own DQN champion 29-1 using the SAME weights, and reported 63%/83%/97% at 25/150/400 sims — roughly +590 Elo from search alone, no retraining. Meanwhile the user-supplied MaskablePPO repo has a demonstrated *ceiling*: after 51.8M steps its generation-vs-generation win rate oscillates 0.36-0.64 around 0.5 (pure noise at n=80) and its "Elo" is a self-referential K-factor chain anchored on an arbitrary 1200. That is not a starting point to scale; it is a measured plateau on a nearly identical card economy.

**2. Splendor's decisive skills are exactly what search computes and what a policy net must memorize.**
The game is won by short-horizon combinatorial checks: "this take leaves me at 10 tokens with 3 reserved and nothing affordable"; "if I don't reserve this card the opponent buys it and hits 15 next turn"; "the final round is triggered — do I have the tiebreak on fewer cards?"; "in TEAM mode does this purchase *revoke* our own final round?". None of these are long-horizon credit-assignment problems; all are 1-4 ply lookahead facts over a branching factor I measured at **median 8, mean 11.7**. Splendor-Zero needed a hand-coded 10x prior boost on blocking-reserve actions precisely because their net could not see this; search sees it for free. Our deadlock measurements make this concrete: **11-33% of greedy-bot games end with the acting seat having no legal move**, and because our server has no PASS, that means resignation (score 0). A policy net has to amortize deadlock avoidance into weights over millions of games; a 1-ply search eliminates it in one forward pass.

**3. On THIS hardware, PPO leaves most of the machine idle; AlphaZero converts idle FLOPs into better targets.**
This is the argument that flips the usual calculus. Every profiled project chose PPO or CPU-MCTS because their environment was the bottleneck: chlligence ran 22 CPU envs with batch-1 opponent forwards; Splendor-Zero was CPU-only with a 0.15-0.8M MLP; cestpasphoto hardcodes `device='cpu'` and calls its small-net/many-sims design explicitly anti-GPU. Our situation is inverted. I measured **29-37 us per full MCTS simulation in pure Python**; 56 actor cores therefore supply ~1.9M simulations/s of tree work, which needs ~1-2 A100s of leaf evaluation to feed. PPO on the same node would run the env at 65k plies/s/core, generate a few hundred thousand transitions per rollout, and then do a handful of gradient steps — the A100s would be idle >90% of the time. AlphaZero is the only one of the candidate algorithms that can *spend* 4xA100 on decision quality rather than on a marginally larger batch.

**4. Multi-player credit assignment favours search-based targets.**
Our required modes include 3p/4p free-for-all, 1v2 with asymmetric thresholds (solo >=15 vs duo combined >=34, winner by larger excess), and 2v2 with a revocable final round. PPO's GAE over an interleaved multi-seat trajectory with a sparse terminal reward is weak here, and the profiles show the failure concretely: chlligence's elegant terminal-reward-negation trick depends on Phi being strictly anti-symmetric, which holds for 2 players and symmetric 2v2 but **provably fails for 3-4 player free-for-all** — their own profile flags this. AlphaZero's value target is (search estimate, game outcome); it needs no anti-symmetry, no zero-sum assumption, and no potential function. A per-seat value vector with max^n backup handles all five modes with one mechanism.

**5. It removes reward shaping, which is the main portability hazard.**
To make PPO work on Splendor, everyone ends up hand-shaping: chlligence stacks PBRS + a board-demand vector + a build bonus + a block/restore bonus + a hoarding penalty. Their hoarding penalty exists ONLY to paper over auto-discard, which our variant does not have. Their PBRS policy-invariance claim does not even hold in their own code (the potential is measured before the opponent replies, so the telescoping sum does not close). Those weights are per-mode, non-transferable, and every one is a place a one-shot autonomous implementation can silently go wrong. AlphaZero needs a terminal outcome function and nothing else — and our engine already computes the exact terminal outcome for all five modes including resign and timeout.

**6. The variant hands us three structural gifts that make AZ unusually cheap here.**
(a) The hard 10-token cap with no discards halves the branching factor versus standard Splendor (11.7 vs Rinascimento's measured 22-28) and deletes the entire discard sub-turn machinery — our 65-action space is already atomic-per-turn and cross-validated. (b) I verified the card and tile tables are **exactly closed under the C5 cyclic colour rotation** (`addCycle` generates all 5 rotations of every template; the 10 tiles are 5 rotations of "4+4 adjacent" and 5 of "3+3+3"), giving a free 5x data augmentation whose action permutation touches only indices 0-29 (board/reserve/buy/tile action indices are slot-based and therefore invariant) — plus a 5x test-time ensemble at deployment. (c) The Python engine is already fast enough (65k plies/s/core) that no C++/numba rewrite is needed for phase 1, removing the single largest schedule risk in the plan.

**7. Deployment cost is a non-issue, so there is no reason to give up search at inference.**
A 13M-parameter MLP is 26 MB in fp16 on a 4 GB card. At my measured 30 us/sim, 6,000-10,000 simulations with 16 determinizations fits comfortably inside a 2-second budget on a desktop CPU + 3060. Any design that trains a search-free policy is throwing away ~+500 Elo that the deployment hardware would give us for free.

### architecture
**Single mode-conditioned policy-value network, ~13M parameters, pre-LN residual MLP over a hand-engineered flat information-set vector. Content-addressed cards, NO card-id embeddings.**

**Input encoding (~860 floats, all in [-1,1], seat-relative, acting player first):**
- *Per-card block (23 floats), used for every card slot*: cost/7 (5), reward-colour one-hot (5), points/5 (1), tier one-hot (3), per-colour shortfall after my discounts /7 (5), total gold needed /5 (1), affordable-now flag (1), turns-to-afford = max(ceil(sum_shortfall/3), max_shortfall)/6 (1), slot-empty flag (1).
- 12 board slots x 23 = 276 (tier-major, matching action indices 30-41 / 45-56).
- Own 3 reserved x 23 = 69.
- Up to 3 opponents x 3 reserved slots x 25 = 225 (23 + `is_known` + `is_deck_reserved`; an unknown card is zeroed with only tier + occupied set — the Splendor-Zero encoding).
- 4 player blocks x 28 = 112: gems/supply (6), discounts/7 (5), score/15, cards/20, reserved count/3, tiles/3, resigned flag, is-self, is-teammate, is-solo-role, seat-offset one-hot (4), excess over own threshold, clock remaining (unused in training, zeroed).
- 5 tile slots x 18 = 90: requirement/4 (5), my card-distance per colour /4 (5), my total distance, available flag, per-opponent min distance (3), qualifies-now flag, slot-empty.
- **Public deck composition (48)**: per tier, remaining-card counts bucketed by (reward colour x points bucket) 3x5x3 = 45, plus per-tier remaining/deck size (3). This is legitimately public (all 90 cards are known and everything dealt is visible) and no profiled project encodes it — it is what lets the net reason about what refills are likely.
- Global (40): bank supply/max (6), mode one-hot (3), team layout, num_players one-hot (3), turn/60, final-round-triggered flag, final-round-triggered-by seat-relative (4), plies until turn returns to round leader /n, revocable-final-round flag, per-side threshold progress (4), pending-tile-choice flag, phase one-hot.

**Trunk:** `LayerNorm(860) -> Linear(860,768) -> 10 x PreLN residual block[LN -> Linear(768,768) -> GELU -> Linear(768,768)] -> LN`. ~12.6M params.

**Heads (all off the 768 trunk):**
- **Policy**: `Linear(768,65)`, masked additively with -1e9 BEFORE log-softmax, in the graph (cestpasphoto's trick — keeps log-probs, entropy and KL targets consistent and makes the exported model incapable of proposing an illegal move). ~50k params.
- **Value**: `Linear(768,256) -> GELU -> Linear(256,4) -> tanh`. A **per-seat vector**, padded to 4 seats, in seat-relative order (index 0 = acting seat). MCTS backup reads entry 0 at each node and rotates the vector on the way up (`np.roll`, cestpasphoto MCTS.py:175-183). This is the single change that makes 2p/3p/4p/1v2/2v2 one codebase.
- **Aux score head**: `Linear(768,256) -> GELU -> Linear(256,4)`, predicting final score per seat / 20. KataGo-style auxiliary; gives dense gradient long before the sparse outcome does.
- **Aux stuck head**: `Linear(768,256) -> GELU -> Linear(256,4)` logits, predicting "this seat ends the game with no legal move". Motivated directly by my measurement that 11-33% of greedy games end this way — it is the single most common catastrophic failure mode and deserves its own supervised signal.

Total ~13.1M params, 26 MB fp16.

**Loss**: `CE(improved_policy, masked_log_softmax) + 1.0 * MSE(value, z_vector) + 0.15 * MSE(score_aux) + 0.15 * BCE(stuck_aux)`. AdamW, lr 2e-4 with 2k-step warmup then cosine to 2e-5, weight decay 1e-4, grad-clip 1.0, bf16 autocast.

**Why an MLP and not a transformer/set encoder**: the card slots are permutation-structured and a per-card token encoder with a shared per-card policy head (cestpasphoto's `StructuredPolicyHead`, Splendor-Zero's slot indexing) is the theoretically nicer shape. But (a) our C5 augmentation already supplies the symmetry that matters most, (b) the 65-action head is only 50k of 13M params so the "flat head is 84% of parameters" problem BreckEmert hit does not arise, and (c) for a one-shot autonomous implementation that must train first time, a pre-LN residual MLP has near-zero failure surface at batch 4096 on an A100. Keep the entity-token trunk as a Phase-8 A/B if arena Elo stalls; do not start there.

### env_design
**The env already exists and has already passed the hard gate — this is the biggest de-risking fact in the whole project.** `/home/user/08162026/splendor_ai/splendor_ai/rules/` is a bit-exact Python mirror of `server/gameLogic.js` (1,296-line `engine.py` + `actions.py` + `cards.py` + `view.py`, 116 pytest tests), and `docs/PROGRESS.md` records the cross-validation as **16,000 games / 1.27M steps across all six configurations with 0 mismatches, two-way legal-set checked, at 73k steps/s**. What follows is what to add on top, not what to rebuild.

**Action space (already fixed, do not renumber)**: 65 atomic-per-turn actions — 0-9 take-3-distinct (C(5,3)), 10-19 take-2-distinct, 20-24 take-1 (forced short take), 25-29 take-2-same, 30-41 reserve face-up (`30+tier0*4+slot`), 42-44 reserve deck top, 45-56 buy face-up, 57-59 buy own reserved, 60-64 CHOOSE_TILE. This is right and I would not change it. Three things to note: (a) it is atomic, so the multi-message server protocol (SELECT_GEM xk -> TAKE_GEMS_CONFIRMED; ENTER_RESERVE -> RESERVE_CARD/RESERVE_FROM_DECK) is expanded only at the worker boundary — legal because ENTER_RESERVE's gold grant and non-cancellability are fully determined by the atomic choice; (b) CHOOSE_TILE is a genuine second decision node with the SAME acting seat, which the search must treat as a same-player edge (Splendor-Zero's `same_player` flag on every PathStep — negate the value on backup only when the turn actually changes hands); (c) there is deliberately NO pass action, matching the server.

**Observation / hidden information**: `view.py` already models the exact asymmetry — `reserved_public[i]` distinguishes board-reserved (publicly seen leaving the market, therefore visible to everyone) from deck-reserved (identity hidden, tier public). The encoder consumes `public_view(state, seat)` and nothing else. **Never encode `state.decks` or an opponent's hidden reserved card id.** Deck *composition* (the multiset of unseen cards) IS public and should be encoded as the 48-float bucket histogram described above; deck *order* is not.

**Determinizer** (the one genuinely new env component): `determinize(state, seat, rng) -> GameState` that (1) collects the unseen multiset = all 90 cards minus (board, all tableaus, all public reserves, the viewer's own reserves, the viewer's own known deck-reserves), (2) partitions it by tier, (3) assigns each opponent hidden deck-reserve a uniformly random unseen card of the announced tier, (4) shuffles the remainder into the three decks respecting the public `deck_counts`. Seed it from a small fixed magic-constant table so a given (universe, node) pair is reproducible within one search (cestpasphoto's `magic_seeds` trick, MCTS.py:14).

**Speed strategy** — measured, not guessed:
- `clone` 3.2 us (309k/s), `legal_mask` 6.2 us (162k/s), full MCTS simulation incl. tree ops **28.9 us @32 sims / 33.6 us @64 / 37.1 us @128**. Random full games: 990/766/656 games/s at 2p/3p/4p; ~65-69k plies/s/core across all modes.
- **This is fast enough. Do not rewrite in C++/numba for Phase 1.** 56 actor cores x 33k sims/s = 1.9M sims/s of tree work, which already saturates 1-2 A100s of leaf evaluation.
- Optimizations that ARE worth doing, in order: (1) reuse a preallocated `bytearray` for the mask instead of a fresh list (`legal_mask` is 6.2 us and is called every node); (2) keep the MCTS tree as flat parallel arrays indexed by node id, not per-node objects; (3) batch leaves ACROSS the ~24 concurrent games inside one actor process rather than using virtual loss within one tree — this is exactly correct with no virtual-loss bias and is simpler; (4) `OMP_NUM_THREADS=OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=MKL_NUM_THREADS=1` set via `os.environ.setdefault` BEFORE any import, plus `torch.set_num_threads(1)` inside every worker (chlligence scripts/train.py:23-25 — 56 actors x 64 cores would otherwise be 3,584 threads on 64 cores).
- Only if Phase 6 shows the CPU as the bottleneck: port `apply` + `legal_mask` + `clone` to a numba jitclass over a single flat int8 array (cestpasphoto's design), which should buy 5-20x. Budget this as a contingency, not a plan item.

**The no-legal-move terminal is a first-class state, not an edge case.** I measured 12.2/25.5/39.1/42.2% incidence under random play and 18.5/11.3/31.0/33.2% under greedy, at 2p/3p/4p/TEAM. The cause is arithmetic: total tokens are 5x5+5 = 30 = 3x10 at 3p and 5x7+5 = 40 = 4x10 at 4p, so global exhaustion is exactly reachable (2p is 25 > 20 and therefore safe). `E.is_stuck()` already detects it. Required semantics: **a stuck seat resigns** (matching the server, which has no pass) and the terminal value is computed by the engine's real `resign()` path — gems returned, cards discarded, score 0 — then scored under the current mode. In TEAM that is catastrophic for the partner too, and the value net must learn it as such. Never invent a pass action to make the env tidier; that would train a policy the server cannot execute.

**Validation against Node — keep it as a permanent CI gate, not a one-time check.** Extend `validation/` with: (a) the existing 2,000-games x 6-configuration random sweep, run in CI on every encoder/engine change; (b) a targeted corpus for the states random play under-samples — 8/9/10 tokens with partial supply (forced short takes), ENTER_RESERVE when a tier's deck is empty and all four face-up slots are gone, multi-tile CHOOSE_TILE, TEAM final-round revocation, ONE_V_TWO irrevocable final round, resign and timeout mid-final-round; (c) real replay JSONs from `server/replayStore.js` fed through the Python engine; (d) an assertion that the 65-bit legal mask and the set of actions Node accepts are equal in BOTH directions on every step (already done — keep it). The chlligence lesson applies directly: two implementations that share your misreading agree with each other, so only Node counts.

### multi_mode_plan
**One network, one training run, five configurations mixed — with optional per-mode fine-tuning at the very end. This is both the strength-maximizing and the artifact-minimizing choice.**

**Why one net.** ~90% of Splendor skill (card economy, affordability, tile distance, deadlock avoidance, blocking, engine-vs-points timing) is mode-independent. Training five separate nets divides the data five ways and forgoes all transfer; the 2p configuration in particular is the cheapest teacher (66 plies vs 105) and its lessons transfer directly. cestpasphoto's repo is the cautionary example: `NUMBER_PLAYERS` is a module-level constant, so they shipped three separate models with three separate observation shapes and zero transfer between them, and their 3p/4p configs got materially less compute (400 sims, 1 epoch) than their 2p one.

**Conditioning.** The mode is not an afterthought — it is encoded as (a) mode one-hot (INDIVIDUAL / ONE_V_TWO / TEAM), (b) num_players one-hot, (c) team layout flag, (d) per-seat role flags (`is_self`, `is_teammate`, `is_solo`), (e) **per-side threshold-progress features**: my excess over MY threshold and each side's excess over ITS threshold. The last one is what lets a single value head express three different win conditions. Player slots are padded to 4 with a validity mask and ordered seat-relative to the actor, so 2p/3p/4p share the same tensor shape.

**Reward / value targets per mode** (all computed exactly by the engine's real terminal code, never approximated):
- *INDIVIDUAL 2/3/4p*: rank-based, not win/lose. `z_i = 1 - 2*(rank_i - 1)/(n-1)`, ties share the average. **Do not use +1-to-winner / -1-to-everyone-else** — that is what cestpasphoto and SIMPLE do, and it gives two of four seats an identical target in every 4p game, destroying the signal that distinguishes "close second" from "hopeless last". Ranking uses the real tiebreak: score, then FEWER cards.
- *ONE_V_TWO*: solo needs >=15, duo needs combined >=34, winner is the larger excess over its OWN threshold, ties possible. `z = +1` to the winning side's seat(s), `-1` to the losing side, `0` on a tie. The solo seat is seat 0 and always starts, so seat identity is genuinely load-bearing here — **do not randomize it away**; instead expose the role flag and let self-play train both sides of the same network.
- *TEAM 2v2*: both teammates receive the identical team result. Because the value vector's entries for the two teammates are identical and PUCT backs up the acting seat's entry, cooperation emerges with no communication channel and no explicit credit assignment. The revocable final round must be modelled as recomputed state (a stateless predicate re-evaluated each turn), not a latched flag — the engine already does this; the observation carries both `final_round_triggered` and `revocable`.

**Mixture schedule** (sampled per game by the actor):
- Phase 5 (bootstrap/early): 60% ind2, 15% team4, 15% ovt3, 5% ind3, 5% ind4 — 2p games are 40% shorter and give the fastest early signal.
- Phase 6-7 (main): 25% ind2, 25% ovt3, 25% team4, 10% ind3, 15% ind4. Weighted toward the two asymmetric modes because they are explicitly required and have the least transfer-in.

**Specialists**: only as a Phase 8 fine-tune from the shared checkpoint, 6-12h each, and only shipped if the fine-tuned model beats the shared model by >=55% over >=2,000 paired-seed arena games in that mode. Expect the shared model to win 2p/3p/4p and the fine-tunes to win marginally in ovt3 and team4. Ship whichever wins per mode; the deployment worker loads one file and selects a head-config by mode, so "one artifact" is preserved either way.

**One explicit warning about self-play in TEAM mode**: shared-weight teammates can converge on private conventions that do not survive contact with a differently-trained partner. Mitigate by drawing the two teammate seats independently from the opponent mixture in 25% of TEAM games (i.e. sometimes your partner is an older checkpoint), and by adding "win rate when partnered with a random historical checkpoint" as a standing arena metric.

### training_plan_4xA100
**PBS request**: `-q ai -l select=1:ngpus=4:ncpus=64:mem=440GB`, walltime per job at the queue maximum (typically 24h at NSCC) with mandatory checkpoint/resume — the run spans ~8-10 jobs, so resume must restore (weights, optimizer state, replay-window index, generation counter, arena ratings, RNG streams).

**Process layout — actor/learner split with dedicated inference servers, NOT DDP-with-inline-actors.**
- **GPU 0**: 1 learner process. Batch 4096, bf16 autocast, AdamW.
- **GPUs 1,2,3**: 3 inference-server processes. Each holds a read-only copy of the current weights (hot-swapped from a shared file every ~500 learner steps — chlligence's `set_opponent` bytes-push pattern, which avoids process respawn), serves `(obs[B,860] float16, mask[B,65] bool) -> (logits[B,65], value[B,4], aux)` at GPU batch 2048, aggregated from many actor requests.
- **56 actor processes**, pinned one per core, each driving **24 concurrent games in lockstep**: advance one MCTS simulation per game, collect 24 leaves, issue ONE request, apply results, backup. Leaf batching across games instead of virtual loss within one tree — exactly correct, no bias to undo, and much simpler than Splendor-Zero's tree-parallel/virtual-loss machinery.
- **4 cores reserved**: 3 for the inference servers' host threads, 1 for the learner feeder.
- **Transport**: shared-memory ring buffers (POSIX shm + numpy views), not pickle/queue. Replay is a preallocated shm ring of 40M augmented samples written by actors, sampled uniformly by the learner.

**Deliberate non-use of DDP**: a 13M-param MLP needs ~4.2 ms for fwd+bwd at batch 4096 on one A100 (~240 steps/s, ~1M samples/s consumed). The actors produce ~54k augmented samples/s. **A single learner GPU is already 15-20x oversupplied**; adding DDP would buy nothing and would add NCCL/rendezvous failure modes to a run that must work first time. Keep the learner DDP-*ready* (wrap in `DistributedDataParallel` behind a `world_size>1` branch) and switch to a 2-learner/2-inference split only if Phase 8 scales the net past ~40M params.

**Measured and derived throughput** (all CPU numbers measured on this box; GPU numbers derived from FLOP counts, so verify in Phase 4):
| quantity | value |
| --- | --- |
| MCTS sim cost, pure Python, incl. tree ops | **28.9-37.1 us** (measured) |
| sims/s/core | 27-35k (measured) |
| sims/s, 56 actors, theoretical | 1.85M |
| sims/s, after 2x IPC/serialization derate | **~0.9M** |
| mean sims/move under PCR (0.25 x 48 + 0.75 x 12) | **21** |
| self-play moves/s node-wide | **~43k** (plan against 20-30k) |
| completed games/s (mixture, ~90 plies mean) | **~480** (plan against 250-350) |
| recorded training positions/s (25% full-search only) | **~11k** |
| after 5x C5 colour augmentation | **~54k samples/s** |
| NN leaf evaluations/s required | ~0.9M -> **300k/s per inference GPU** |
| A100 capacity, 13M MLP, bf16, batch 2048 | ~1-2.5M evals/s (2x107 GFLOP per 4096-batch) -> **3-8x headroom** |
| learner steps/s at batch 4096 | run at ~40 (capacity ~240) -> 164k samples/s consumed, **replay ratio ~3x** |

The headroom is deliberate: it is the budget for raising sims/move (48 -> 96) or net size (13M -> 40M) in Phase 8 once the loop is proven.

**Replay window**: rolling generational, 4 generations for the first 5 cycles then +1 every 2 cycles to a cap of 20 (KataGo / Splendor-Zero's `_rolling_replay_window_generations`). Trim whole generations, never individual samples.

**League / opponent mixture** (75% pure latest-vs-latest self-play, 25% mixed):
- In mixed games, sample **each seat independently** from {latest 0.60, uniform historical 0.25, pinned anchor 0.10, scripted greedy 0.05}. Per-seat independence explicitly fixes SIMPLE's flaw (`agents = [opponent] * n_players` puts one identical frozen policy in every opponent seat, which is a far narrower distribution than it looks).
- **Pinned anchors are never evicted.** chlligence evicts the lowest-Elo entry, which under a monotonically drifting rating chain degenerates to "keep the last 30 generations" — discarding exactly the stylistically different opponents that guard against cycling, and a plausible mechanical cause of their plateau.
- Mixed-game data is still trained on: AZ targets are search-derived and remain valid regardless of who occupies the other seats.

**Curriculum**:
1. **Bootstrap (4h, optional but recommended)**: generate ~10M moves with a *NN-free* 2000-sim MCTS teacher using random rollouts (56 cores x 33k sims/s / 2000 = ~920 moves/s -> 10M moves in 3h), then supervised-train the initial net on (visit distribution, outcome) for 1h. This removes the AlphaZero cold-start where a uniform prior makes low-sim Gumbel search nearly useless. This is seal256's recipe and it costs almost nothing.
2. **Gen 0-10 (~12h)**: n=32 sims, 2p-heavy mixture, lr 2e-4.
3. **Gen 10-40 (~3 days)**: n=48, full five-mode mixture, league mixing on.
4. **Gen 40-70 (~3 days)**: n=64-96, lr cosine to 2e-5; optionally widen the trunk with `load_not_strict` sub-block weight copying (cestpasphoto GenericNNetWrapper.py:223-249) so architecture growth does not discard learned weights.
5. **Fine-tune + arena + export (~1 day)**.

**Total: ~7-9 days of node time.** For calibration: 0.9M sims/s x 86,400 = **7.8e10 simulations per day**, which is roughly the size of Splendor-Zero's entire committed 7-day, 44-core SLURM self-play campaign — the one that reached rank 1 on Spendee. We should reach their compute-equivalent inside 24-36 hours, and everything after that is margin.

**Instrumentation that must exist from day one** (fail loudly, not silently):
- Per generation: arena Elo vs the three fixed anchors, mean/median game length per mode, **stuck-rate per mode**, mean cards bought, nobles claimed, gold usage, policy entropy, value MSE, `explained_variance`, aux-head accuracies, fraction of moves where the search's argmax differs from the raw policy argmax (search-value signal), and effective sims evaluated vs requested (Splendor-Zero's `search_slots_drop_*` counters — a search that silently drops simulations invalidates every "N sims" claim).
- Hard assertions: mask never all-zero unless `is_stuck`; every sampled action is legal; `sum(policy_target) == 1`; policy target is exactly zero on illegal actions; observation-encoding version stamped into every checkpoint with a RuntimeError on mismatch (chlligence's `format_version` gate — cheap insurance against silently mixing checkpoints trained under different encodings, a disaster that cost them a whole generation era).

### inference_plan_3060
**Search at inference, always. This is where a large fraction of the final strength lives and the hardware is free.**

**Budget**: the server allows "a few seconds"; target a 2.0 s hard deadline with an anytime search that returns the best action found so far, plus a 1.5 s soft target so network latency never causes a timeout.

**Configuration**:
- Model in **fp16, ~26 MB** (13M params). Total VRAM including CUDA context and activation buffers at batch 32: **<1.0 GB of the 4 GB**. Non-issue.
- **PUCT/Gumbel search, ~6,000-10,000 simulations** with leaf batching at B=32 across the tree (virtual loss applied on descent and rolled back — Splendor-Zero `native_mcts.cpp:2441; the same-weights measurement there was 0.75 -> 0.18 s/move at batch 8, 4.1x). Arithmetic: 8,000 sims x 30 us CPU = 240 ms of tree work; 250 GPU batches x ~0.3 ms = 75 ms; with 4-8 CPU worker threads for tree descent, comfortably inside 1.5 s.
- **K=16 root determinizations**, re-sampled per simulation on the shared action-path-keyed tree. Add Splendor-Zero's small random PUCT penalty on RESERVE_FROM_DECK at the root (uniform in [0, 0.02/0.06/0.12] by tier) to offset the determinizer's false certainty about the deck top.
- **C5 rotation ensemble at the root**: evaluate the root position under all 5 colour rotations and average the policy priors (after un-permuting indices 0-29) and the value. 5 extra forward passes, sub-millisecond, and it is a genuine free strength gain from the symmetry I verified. Optionally extend to every leaf if latency allows.
- **Exact final-round solver**: once `final_round_triggered` is set, the game ends within at most one more round (<= n plies). With median branching 8, that subtree is <= ~8^4 ~ 4k nodes per determinization; solve it EXACTLY by alpha-beta negamax/max^n with a transposition table, averaged over 16 determinizations (~65k nodes ~ 1 s in Python, far less with pruning). This makes the endgame — including the fewer-cards tiebreak, the ONE_V_TWO excess-over-threshold comparison, and TEAM final-round revocation — provably optimal rather than approximately optimal. Splendor-Zero built this and never wired it up; our variant's return-to-round-leader rule makes it worth more than it was to them.
- **Hard stuck filter**: before returning, verify the chosen action does not lead to a position where our own next turn has no legal move (one `apply` + `legal_mask` per candidate = ~10 us x 12 candidates). Never play into a provable deadlock unless every legal action does. Given the 11-33% deadlock rate I measured under greedy play, this alone is a large practical strength gain and it is deterministic.
- **Shadow state**: the server's `clientViewForPlayer` hides opponents' reserved cards without distinguishing board-reserved (publicly seen) from deck-reserved (truly hidden). The worker must maintain its own shadow state from the action log to reconstruct `reserved_public` — Splendor-Zero's `spendee/shadow_state.py` pattern, and exactly what `docs/PLAN.md` Sec.3 already anticipates. Feed the reconstructed information set to the encoder, not the raw client view.
- **Fallback ladder** (each must be tested): GPU unavailable -> CPU inference with 800 sims (~40 ms tree + ~200 ms CPU NN, still inside budget); model load failure -> the 2000-sim NN-free MCTS; total failure -> the greedy heuristic bot; no legal action -> resign.
- **Determinism**: seed per-move from `(gameId, turnNumber)` so a move is reproducible for debugging. Do NOT do what Splendor-Zero did and draw the anti-clairvoyance penalty from a `thread_local mt19937(random_device{})`, which made every search non-reproducible regardless of the seed passed in.
- **Evaluate what you deploy**: run the arena with the identical search configuration and the identical stochasticity setting used at deployment. chlligence evaluated with `deterministic=False` and shipped `deterministic=True`; Splendor-Zero's deployed search path silently falls back to plain MCTS on an ISMCTS failure. Both mean the measured agent is not the shipped agent.

- Bounded-FIFO frozen-snapshot league with PFSP weights max((1 - winrate_EMA)^p, eps) and unmeasured opponents at max priority — BreckEmert RL/vrpo_league.py:58-115. Use it for the 25% mixed-game slice, sampling each seat independently.
- Rinascimento's 18-event taxonomy (take token / reserve hidden / reserve board / receive tile / receive points ...) as auxiliary prediction targets — game/log/converters/IdentityConverter.java. Dense, cheap, low-dimensional signal for the sparse early phase; their result was that an 18-D trajectory summary beat a 192-D state vector as a rollout evaluator.

### risks
- **AlphaZero cold start.** With a uniform prior and only 32-48 simulations, early Gumbel search barely improves on the prior, and the first few generations can crawl. MITIGATION: the NN-free 2000-sim MCTS bootstrap (3h of CPU, 10M moves) plus n=64 sims for generations 0-3. GO/NO-GO: generation 3 must beat the greedy anchor >=80%.
- **GPU throughput numbers are derived, not measured.** My 1-2.5M evals/s per A100 comes from a FLOP count (2 x 13M x B), not from a benchmark — there is no GPU in this sandbox. If real throughput is 5x lower, we drop to ~180k evals/s per GPU and must either shrink the net, raise the batch, or move a learner GPU to inference. MITIGATION: Phase 4 is a pure throughput benchmark with an explicit numeric gate before any long run.
- **Pure-Python MCTS at 30 us/sim may degrade under real load.** My measurement used a stub evaluator and a single tree; real actors add IPC, serialization, and 24 interleaved trees per process, which will hurt cache locality. If effective throughput falls below ~10k sims/s/core, the numba port of apply/legal_mask/clone (cestpasphoto's flat-int8-array jitclass design) becomes a schedule item, not a contingency. Budget 3-5 days for it.
- **PIMC strategy fusion.** Root determinization assumes each simulation's hidden state is known, which over-values plans that depend on knowing an opponent's hidden reserve or a deck top. In our variant this is mild (no bluffing channel; the unseen multiset is public; deck-reserves are rare) but it is real. MITIGATION: K=16 universes on a shared tree, the deck-reserve PUCT penalty, and a Phase-7 measurement — build a 'clairvoyant' agent that sees the true state and measure the strength gap; if it exceeds ~50 Elo, escalate to true information-set keying (Splendor-Zero's InfoSetKey over an observer-canonical raw state).
- **Rank-based value targets in 3p/4p can encourage second-place play.** A z that rewards rank rather than winning may make the agent settle. MITIGATION: keep the rank target but verify in the arena on WIN RATE, not mean z; if outright wins lag, sharpen the mapping toward winner-take-most.
- **TEAM self-play conventions.** Shared-weight teammates can converge on private protocols that collapse when partnered with a human or a different checkpoint. MITIGATION: 25% of TEAM games draw the two teammate seats independently from the opponent mixture; track 'win rate partnered with a random historical checkpoint' as a standing metric.
- **The stuck terminal may be gameable in the wrong direction.** In TEAM and ONE_V_TWO, deliberately forcing an OPPONENT into a stuck state (denying tokens and board cards) is a legitimate and possibly dominant strategy the agent will discover. That is fine competitively but produces very long, degenerate games. MITIGATION: monitor mean game length and stuck-rate-by-victim per generation; this is a diagnostic to watch, not a bug to suppress.
- **NSCC walltime fragmentation.** ~8-10 chained 24h jobs means checkpoint/resume is on the critical path, and a resume bug silently restarting from generation 0 would be invisible for a day. MITIGATION: resume must be tested by killing and restarting a job in Phase 4; the generation counter and cumulative sample count are logged and asserted monotone.
- **Licensing.** Splendor-Zero, chlligence, seal256, BreckEmert and Nikola995 have NO LICENSE file (all-rights-reserved by default; Splendor-Zero's README claims MIT with no LICENSE present). SIMPLE is GPL-3.0. Only cestpasphoto (MIT, with upstream suragnair attribution) and Rinascimento (MIT) are safe to copy from. MITIGATION: re-implement every idea; copy literal code only from the two MIT repos and attribute both upstream and fork.
- **Gumbel AlphaZero is less battle-tested in hobby code than PUCT.** If the sequential-halving implementation is subtly wrong, training degrades in a way that looks like slow progress rather than a crash. MITIGATION: implement PUCT first as the base path, gate Gumbel behind a flag, and A/B them in Phase 5 with an explicit arena decision; also assert that the improved policy is a valid distribution and that it puts nonzero mass only on legal actions.
- **Encoder/engine drift after the validation gate.** The engine passed 16,000 games with 0 mismatches, but every future edit can break it. MITIGATION: the Node cross-validation runs in CI on every commit touching splendor_ai/rules or the encoder — not once.
- **The 3060 worker is the least-tested component and sits on the critical path to any real strength measurement.** A move that is legal in Python but rejected by the server (e.g. an ENTER_RESERVE expansion edge case) would be discovered only in production. MITIGATION: Phase 9 runs the worker against a LOCAL Node server for >=500 full games in all five modes with a zero-rejection assertion before anything touches Render.

### phase_order
- **P0 — Freeze the decision and the interfaces (0.5 day).** Write docs/AI_DESIGN.md recording this decision, the 65-action table (already fixed), the encoder layout with a version stamp, the terminal-value function per mode, and the actor/learner/inference contract. GO/NO-GO: the encoder spec is complete enough that the network input width is a constant, and every field traces to something view.py already exposes.
- **P1 — Extend the Node cross-validation and make it CI (1 day).** Keep the existing 16,000-game/1.27M-step sweep; ADD targeted corpora for the states random play under-samples: 8/9/10 tokens with partial supply (forced short takes), ENTER_RESERVE with an empty deck and empty face-up row, multi-tile CHOOSE_TILE, TEAM final-round revocation, ONE_V_TWO irrevocable final round, resign/timeout inside the final round, and every is_stuck position reachable in 200k random games. GO/NO-GO: **0 mismatches, two-way legal-set equality, on >=25,000 games across all six configurations, running green in CI.** This gate already passed once — do not let it silently rot.
- **P2 — Encoder + C5 symmetry + baselines (1.5 days).** Implement encode.py (batched, numpy, ~860 floats), the 65-index colour-rotation permutation table, and three fixed anchors: uniform-random-legal, a 1-ply greedy bot, and a NN-free 2000-sim MCTS. GO/NO-GO: (a) for 100k random states and all k in 0..4, `encode(rotate(s,k))` equals `permute(encode(s),k)` exactly and the legal mask permutes correspondingly; (b) the 2000-sim MCTS anchor beats greedy >=85% and greedy beats random >=95% over paired-seed matches in all five modes; (c) encoder throughput >=200k states/s/core.
- **P3 — Network + learner + the arena, offline (1 day).** Build the 13M net, the masked policy head, the four heads, and the Bradley-Terry/Bayes-Elo arena with paired seeds and full seat rotation. Supervised-train on 2M positions from the MCTS anchor. GO/NO-GO: the supervised net alone (no search) beats greedy >=70% and beats random >=98%; policy top-1 agreement with the teacher's argmax >=45%; value sign accuracy >=65%. **If the supervised net cannot beat greedy, the encoder is wrong — stop and fix it before touching self-play.**
- **P4 — Throughput benchmark on the real node (0.5 day, first PBS job).** Stand up 56 actors + 3 inference servers + 1 learner and measure, with no learning: sims/s/core, sims/s node-wide, GPU evals/s per A100 at batch 2048, learner steps/s at batch 4096, and end-to-end self-play moves/s. Kill and restart the job to prove checkpoint/resume. GO/NO-GO: **>=400k sims/s node-wide, >=250k NN evals/s per inference GPU, >=100 learner steps/s, and a clean resume.** If sims/s misses, escalate the numba port BEFORE burning days of self-play; if GPU evals/s misses, shrink the trunk to 512-wide.
- **P5 — Self-play loop, 2p-heavy, with the Gumbel-vs-PUCT A/B (1.5 days).** Bootstrap from P3, run generations 0-10 at n=32-64 on the 2p-heavy mixture. Run both root-selection schemes as parallel arms for the first 5 generations and pick by arena. GO/NO-GO: **by generation 5, >=70% vs the 2000-sim MCTS anchor in 2p; by generation 10, >=85%, with a monotone Elo curve against the FIXED anchors (not against the previous generation).** Also: stuck-rate in self-play must be falling, and search-argmax-differs-from-policy-argmax must be >15% (if it is near zero, search is contributing nothing and something is wrong).
- **P6 — Full five-mode mixture at scale (3 days).** Generations 10-40 at n=48, league mixing on, per-seat independent opponent sampling, pinned anchors. GO/NO-GO at generation 25: **>=90% vs the MCTS anchor in every one of the five configurations; stuck-rate below 2% in every mode; no seat-win-rate asymmetry beyond what the game's structural first-player advantage explains** (measure it against the anchors, which have no seat preference). Abort criterion: if arena Elo against the fixed anchors is flat for 8 consecutive generations, the loop has plateaued — diagnose (replay window, league diversity, sims/move, net capacity) rather than continuing to burn node hours.
- **P7 — Scale-up and the clairvoyance audit (3 days).** Generations 40-70 at n=64-96; optionally widen the trunk with sub-block weight copying. Build a clairvoyant agent that sees the true deck and hidden reserves and measure the Elo gap versus the information-set agent under identical search settings. GO/NO-GO: gap <=50 Elo (PIMC is adequate; proceed); >50 Elo means escalate to information-set tree keying. Also A/B the C5 root ensemble and the exact final-round solver, each of which must show a measurable arena gain to ship.
- **P8 — Per-mode fine-tune and export (1 day).** Fine-tune the shared checkpoint per mode for 6-12h each. GO/NO-GO: a specialist ships only if it beats the shared model >=55% over >=2,000 paired-seed games in its own mode; otherwise ship the shared net for that mode. Export to a single versioned .pt carrying weights + encoder version + config, plus a TorchScript/ONNX path for the worker.
- **P9 — Worker and end-to-end conformance against a LOCAL Node server (1.5 days).** Run the 3060 worker (or a CPU stand-in) against a local server/index.js for >=500 complete games across all five modes, driving both AI and scripted-human seats. GO/NO-GO: **zero rejected actions, zero timeouts at the 2s budget, zero stuck-resignations, p99 move latency <2.0s, and the worker's reconstructed shadow state matching the server's true reserved_public on every step.** Only then point it at Render.
- **P10 — Standing regression harness (ongoing).** Every subsequent checkpoint runs the same anchored arena, the Node cross-validation, and the behaviour diagnostics (game length, cards bought, tiles claimed, gold usage, stuck rate) before it is allowed to replace the deployed model. This is the single practice whose absence made every profiled project's strength claim unverifiable.

### expected_strength
**Stated against fixed anchors, because no profiled project's self-referential Elo means anything.** Targets at P7 completion:

- vs uniform-random-legal: **>99.5%** in all five modes.
- vs the 1-ply greedy bot: **>98%** in all five modes.
- vs the NN-free 2000-sim MCTS anchor: **>=92%** in 2p, **>=85%** in 3p/4p/ovt/team (multi-player win rates compress toward chance, so compare against a 1/n baseline: 85% at 4p against a 25% chance baseline is a very large margin).
- **Search vs no search, same weights**: I expect **+350 to +600 Elo**, bracketing BreckEmert's measured +590 (63%/83%/97% at 25/150/400 sims) and Splendor-Zero's 29-1 result. This is the number to measure first at P7 because it is the cleanest possible ablation and it directly validates the central premise of this recommendation.
- **Deadlock rate**: from the 11-33% I measured under greedy play down to **<0.5%** for the deployed agent (search's 1-ply stuck filter makes it near-zero by construction).
- **Endgame**: provably optimal once the final round is triggered, via the exact determinized solver.

**Versus humans, honestly.** In 2p I expect clearly superhuman play — the position count is small, the branching factor is 8-12, the endgame is solvable, and we will have spent compute comparable to Splendor-Zero's full campaign within 36 hours. In 4p individual and 2v2 the picture is murkier: outcome variance is higher, so even a much stronger agent posts a lower headline win rate, and coordination conventions in 2v2 may not transfer to a human partner. In 1v2 I expect the agent to be strong as the solo (a pure optimization problem) and somewhat weaker as a duo member (implicit coordination against an asymmetric threshold).

**What I am NOT claiming.** No project in the profiles has a credible human benchmark: Splendor-Zero's 2420 rating is 125 games against a field the bot itself filters by rating; BreckEmert's "48-2 on BGA" is self-reported with a population-average screenshot as evidence; the DiVA thesis's "super-human" is a stated aim, not a result. We will be in the same position unless we build the yardstick, which is why the fixed MCTS anchor and an anchored Bayes-Elo ladder are P2/P3 deliverables rather than afterthoughts. The one absolute statement I am comfortable making up front: **a search-equipped agent trained this way will be far stronger than anything reachable by the MaskablePPO configuration in the user-supplied repo**, because that configuration's own checkpoint metadata shows it flat after 51.8M steps.

### disagreements_with_naive_choice
**I disagree with plain MaskablePPO self-play as the main line, on six specific points. Not because PPO is a bad algorithm, but because on this game and this hardware it is the one that leaves the most strength on the table.**

**1. The evidence for it is a measured ceiling, not a starting point.** chlligence's `checkpoints/pool_index.json` records generations 63-99: win rate against the immediately preceding generation oscillating 0.3625-0.6375 around 0.5 (indistinguishable from noise at n=80, sigma ~5.6%), with several late generations measurably *worse* than their predecessor, and Elo wandering inside a ~10-point band — after 51,814,400 environment steps. Their own tooling documents that an earlier, numerically identical-looking `pool_index.json` was produced by broken evaluation code. Treat 50M steps of MaskablePPO on a near-identical card economy as a demonstrated plateau. Scaling the same recipe onto 4xA100 buys more of the same steps, not a different regime.

**2. It cannot use the hardware.** This is the argument I would press hardest. PPO's compute is env stepping (65k plies/s/core here — the CPUs are the work) plus a modest number of gradient steps on a small net. On 4xA100 the GPUs would sit idle >90% of the time; the only lever is a bigger batch, which does not improve a policy. Every profiled project chose PPO or CPU-MCTS *because their env was the bottleneck* — chlligence ran 22 CPU envs with batch-1 opponent forwards, cestpasphoto hardcodes `device='cpu'` and explicitly calls its small-net/many-sims design anti-GPU, Splendor-Zero has zero CUDA in the repo. Our situation is inverted, and AlphaZero is the only candidate that turns idle A100 FLOPs directly into better training targets.

**3. It forfeits the largest single measured effect in the whole landscape.** Search adds roughly +590 Elo on unchanged weights (BreckEmert, same-weights ablation, 150 sims beating the DQN champion 29-1; 97% at 400 sims). Nothing else in any of the ten profiles comes within an order of magnitude of that effect size. A PPO-only pipeline can recover most of it by bolting search on at inference (option 3, the hybrid) — but then it has paid the search cost anyway and forgone the compounding benefit of search-improved *targets* during training. That is the worst of both worlds.

**4. Splendor's decisive skills are lookahead, and our variant makes that sharper.** The hard 10-token cap with no discards creates a genuinely reachable no-legal-move terminal — I measured 12.2/25.5/39.1/42.2% under random play and 18.5/11.3/31.0/33.2% under a greedy bot at 2p/3p/4p/TEAM, driven by token supply being exactly n x 10 at 3p (30) and 4p (40). A stuck seat must resign for score 0. A model-free policy has to amortize avoidance of this into weights; a 1-ply search eliminates it deterministically. The same applies to blocking (Splendor-Zero needed a hand-coded 10x prior boost precisely because their net could not see one-ply threats), to the final-round arithmetic, and to the fewer-cards tiebreak.

**5. Its reward shaping does not survive contact with our five modes.** Making PPO work here means hand-shaping, and chlligence's stack is the template: PBRS + a board-demand vector + a build bonus + a block/restore bonus + a hoarding penalty. Three problems. (a) The hoarding penalty exists ONLY to paper over auto-discard, which our variant does not have — the mask handles the cap. (b) Their PBRS policy-invariance claim is false in their own code: the potential is measured after the agent's move but before the opponent replies, so the telescoping sum does not close, and their `assert gamma == shaping_gamma` gives false confidence about a guarantee that does not hold. (c) Most seriously, their terminal-reward-negation trick depends on Phi being strictly anti-symmetric, which holds for 2 players and symmetric 2v2 but **provably fails for 3-4 player free-for-all** — their own profile flags this. We need 3p and 4p individual and 1v2. AlphaZero needs no potential function, no anti-symmetry, and no zero-sum assumption: just the exact terminal outcome, which our engine already computes.

**6. Its multi-player credit assignment is the weakest part of the recipe, and we need three multi-player modes.** GAE over an interleaved multi-seat trajectory with a terminal-only reward, across 90-110 plies of which only ~25 are yours, is a hard estimation problem — which is exactly why BreckEmert's own author went chasing Q-boosting variants and reported the critic loss "never improving." AlphaZero replaces long-horizon bootstrapping with (search estimate, observed outcome) and a per-seat value vector; the multi-player generalization is a `np.roll` and a max^n backup.

**Where I DO keep PPO, and where I disagree with the current plan's hybrid framing.** `docs/PLAN.md` Sec.4.2 currently makes masked PPO "Level-1 (must succeed)" and search "Level-2 (optional upgrade)". I would invert that priority. Search-in-the-loop is the main line; masked PPO is retained as (a) an *alternative* bootstrap to the NN-free MCTS teacher, (b) a permanent frozen arena baseline, and (c) a pre-built fallback if the AZ loop misses its P5 gate. The infrastructure cost of keeping both is small — identical actors, encoder, network, and arena, differing only in the target-producer module — and it is the right insurance for a one-shot autonomous implementation. But it should be the parachute, not the aircraft.

**Two further disagreements with the plan as written, both cheap to fix:**
- **`docs/PLAN.md` Sec.4.3 specifies a card-id embedding.** Drop it. Content-addressed cards (cost vector, reward one-hot, points, tier) are what make the C5 colour-rotation augmentation exact — an id embedding is not equivariant under the rotation, so it would silently destroy a verified 5x data multiplier and a free test-time ensemble. It also buys nothing: cards are fully characterized by their content.
- **Sec.4.2 and Sec.4.4 propose separate weights per mode and a PBS script that runs four modes on four GPUs in parallel.** That optimizes experiment throughput at the cost of strength: it divides the data five ways and forgoes all transfer. Train one mode-conditioned network on a mixture of all five configurations in a single run, and fine-tune specialists only at the end, only if they win the arena.
